## 1. Positioning

**For European and UK quantum companies going after a public call: I check eligibility, find the partners you are missing, review your proposal the way an evaluator would, and structure the non-scientific sections. You pay a fixed price per call, my use of AI is declared, and I make no promise of success.**

That sentence leads to three choices:
- **Priced per call, not by subscription.** Quantum calls come in waves. Only about a dozen EU topics a year carry "quantum" in their title. Paying every month while waiting for the next wave makes little sense for an SME.
- **The science stays with your scientists.** I do not write the technical "Excellence" section. I check, organise, cost and review.
- **Transparency first.** I do not yet have a record of funded proposals (section 6), so I publish my results as they come in, in a public register (section 5).

## 2. Who this is for, and who it is not for

**For:**
- **EU and UK quantum start-ups and SMEs** working in computing, sensing, communications or software. Most have a few dozen staff and no grants department.
- **Component and enabling-technology makers** (cryogenics, lasers, photonics, control electronics, materials). Two Chips JU calls target them: QUANTUM-1 and QUANTUM-2, open from 1 December 2026 to 31 March 2027 ([qt.eu](https://qt.eu/funding-opportunities)). So do public procurements of laboratory equipment.
- **Quantum and hybrid-HPC software vendors**, for example for EuroHPC QML-07, which closes on 28 January 2027 at 17:00 CET ([EuroHPC](https://www.eurohpc-ju.europa.eu/quantum-machine-learning_en)).
- **Quantum-sensing companies**, whose reliance on public money is the best documented. In the QED-C survey, 69% receive government R&D funding, and it averages 56% of their financial input ([QED-C 2026](https://quantumconsortium.org/mp-files/quantum-sensing-market-forecast-2026_1.pdf/)).
- **Companies that have already won public funding** and lack hands for the next application.
- **Grant consultancies** that need extra capacity before a deadline (service G, white-label).

**Not for:**
- **Anyone looking for a writer with EIC Accelerator wins.** That is not me (section 6). I do not take on full EIC Accelerator write-ups or jury-interview coaching.
- **Anyone who wants a guarantee of funding.** Nobody can honestly offer one (section 7.3).
- **Anyone who wants AI to write the proposal for them.** Proposals that rely heavily on LLM-generated text score lower and are funded less often ([JRC, March 2026](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/handle/JRC146131)).
- **Projects that would require sending me export-controlled technical data or classified information** (section 7.10).
- **Universities and research organisations with their own grants office**, unless they ask for something specific.
- **US SBIR/STTR applications**, which are out of scope for now.

### 4.2 A. Opportunity brief (free)

- **Purpose.** Show you on one page that an open call may match what you have already been funded for or published, so you can decide whether to look further.
- **Deliverable.** One page in the [fiche-opportunite.md](LIVRABLES/fiche-opportunite.md) format, covering:
  - your past public funding, with sources (CORDIS, UKRI Gateway to Research, etc.);
  - 1–3 open calls and their deadlines;
  - possible reasons for fit;
  - eligibility points to check;
  - an estimate of the effort;
  - official links.
- **Turnaround.** 2 working days on request. I may also send one unprompted.
- **Limits.** One brief per company per call, and at most 3 per quarter. I use only public data about your organisation and collect no personal data.
- **Excluded.** A brief is not an eligibility opinion or a recommendation to apply, and it may contain errors because it relies on public information only.

### 4.3 B. Eligibility and fit note (one call)

- **Purpose.** Tell you within 5 working days whether you can apply, whether you should, and on what conditions, before you commit weeks of work.
- **Deliverable.** A 4–6 page note in the [note-eligibilite.md](LIVRABLES/note-eligibilite.md) format, containing:
  - an **eligibility and admissibility checklist**, based on the official call text and the programme's general annexes. It covers entity type, country, SME status under Recommendation 2003/361/EC, minimum consortium composition, participation restrictions, funding rate, financial capacity, and ethics and security requirements;
  - a **fit score out of 100**, with the reasoning behind each criterion;
  - **red flags**;
  - a **reasoned go / go-with-conditions / no-go decision**;
  - an **effort estimate and backward schedule**;
  - a **cost-benefit sanity check**.
- **You provide.** A one-page questionnaire, up-to-date articles of association, headcount and key figures, a simplified cap table (for SME status), and a 10-line project idea with no patentable detail.
- **Turnaround.** 5 working days. **If the note is late, you get a full refund.**
- **Price.** €490, plus €90 for each extra partner whose eligibility I check (up to 5).
- **Excluded.**
  - Any guarantee that the funding body will agree with my reading. Where there is serious doubt, I refer you to the NCP or the call helpdesk and say so in the note.
  - Legal or tax advice.
  - Any check of the project's scientific validity.

### 4.4 C. Partner and consortium search memo

- **Purpose.** Complete a consortium, or fill a missing role: end user, supplier, HPC centre, research organisation, pilot line. Standard Horizon Europe RIA and IA actions usually require at least three independent entities from three different countries. Check this rule for each call.
- **Deliverable.** A memo in the [memo-consortium.md](LIVRABLES/memo-consortium.md) format, containing:
  - a **gap analysis**: the roles the call requires or expects, compared with the roles you already have;
  - a **long list of 15–30 organisations** from public data (CORDIS, the Funding & Tenders partner search, ecosystem maps), each with evidence such as past projects and their links;
  - a **short list of 5–8 organisations**, each with its rationale and its risks (competition, intellectual property, geographic concentration);
  - a **complementarity matrix**;
  - **approach-message templates**, which you send yourself;
  - a **first-meeting agenda**;
  - **pointers on the consortium agreement**, such as the DESCA model. These are not legal advice.
- **Turnaround.** 8 working days.
- **Price.** €990 for 1–2 roles to fill; €1,490 for 3 or more.
- **Excluded.**
  - Any guarantee that a partner will say yes.
  - Contacting anyone on your behalf without a written mandate.
  - Negotiating or drafting the consortium agreement.
  - Personal data. The memo names organisations, not people; you identify contacts through official channels or your own network.
  - Commissions from suggested partners. I accept none (section 7.7).

### 4.5 D. "Red team" review against the official evaluation criteria

- **Purpose.** Read your draft the way an evaluator will, before an evaluator does, and rank the fixes by their likely effect on your score.

For Horizon Europe and the Joint Undertakings the criteria are Excellence, Impact, and Quality and efficiency of implementation.   - **comments in the document**;
  - a **one-hour debrief** by video call.
- **You provide.** The draft, the call text, the budget and the partner list, at least 12 working days before the deadline. With less notice, I add 30% if I have capacity; otherwise I decline.

No service guarantees that you will win funding or a contract. Public success rates are low:

Evaluators and the funding body decide. Every B note includes a cost-benefit check, and if the effort outweighs the likely benefit I say so in writing.

### 7.4 Confidentiality
- **Non-disclosure agreement.** We sign a mutual NDA before I receive any non-public information. I provide one by default, and I can sign yours instead.
- **Limited use.** I use your information only for your assignment and never reuse it for another client.
- **No third parties by default.** I use no subcontractors by default, and any third party needs your prior written approval.
- **Storage.** I keep your documents with an EU-based host, protected by two-factor authentication.
- **Deletion.** I delete your documents 90 days after the assignment ends. The exceptions are records the law requires me to keep, such as invoices, and the register entry if you have authorised it.
- **Unpublished ideas.** I never put unpublished, potentially patentable content into a third-party AI tool without your specific written consent. The Dutch NCP (RVO) advises never to put information that could lead to a patent into such tools, because doing so could count as disclosure ([RVO](https://english.rvo.nl/topics/horizon-europe/how-use-ai-grant-proposal-writing)).

### 7.5 AI-use policy
- **Consent first, opt-out at no extra cost.** I use AI only with the consent you give on ordering, as the EIC code of conduct requires. You can opt out at no extra cost; turnaround then increases by 2 working days.
- **What AI is used for:**
  - desk research on public sources;
  - matching your company to calls;
  - compliance checks: page counts, mandatory sections, consistency of figures;
  - table skeletons;
  - comparing versions;
  - working translations.
- **What AI does not do:**
  - It does not write final text. A human writes or rewrites everything you receive.
  - It does not produce any citation, reference or figure that has not been checked against its source.
  - It does not receive patentable ideas without your specific consent.
  - It does not run on consumer tools. I use only business plans or APIs whose contract terms exclude training on your data, with a data processing agreement.
- **Transparency.** Each deliverable comes with an AI-use log listing the tools, the tasks and the data shared. I also supply a declaration paragraph ready to paste in wherever the programme requires one. Horizon Europe allows generative AI, but applicants must say which tools they used and how, and they remain "fully responsible" for the content, including the parts the AI produced ([standard expert briefing v13.0, Dec 2025](https://www.leuphana.de/fileadmin/user_upload/forschung/Files/transparenz/standard-briefing-slides-for-experts_he_en.pdf); [RVO](https://english.rvo.nl/topics/horizon-europe/how-use-ai-grant-proposal-writing); [ERA Forum guidelines](https://research-and-innovation.ec.europa.eu/news/all-research-and-innovation-news/guidelines-responsible-use-generative-ai-research-developed-european-research-area-forum-2024-03-20_en)). For UK competitions, I check the funder's current policy each time.
- **Why the caution.** The JRC found that company proposals relying extensively on LLM-generated text score lower and are funded less often, while partial assistance shows little effect ([JRC146131](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/handle/JRC146131)).

What follows from these benchmarks:
- **B note (€490).**
  - It costs about half of BW&CO's cheapest published review ($1,000 + 1%) and stays below the level at which a purchase needs committee approval.
  - It takes about 4 hours of human work, or roughly €120 an hour.
  - It competes with the NCP, which advises for free. The value I add is a sourced written note, delivered in 5 working days and refunded if late.
- **D review (€690–€1,890).**
  - It costs 5–15% of a full write-up (€5,000–€30,000 for the EIC; $13,000–$18,000 for SBIR).
  - It takes about 6–15 hours, or €115–150 an hour.
- **E packs (€3,400 and €4,900).**
  - They cost one third to one half of a full write-up, because the science is excluded.
  - The Horizon Europe pack takes about 30–40 hours.
  - The shared formula's 2% sits at the bottom of the observed European range, and it is capped.
- **Service F.** I found no published market price for tender-response support. Prices are therefore based on estimated hours (12–20) and **remain to be validated** with the first clients.
- **Service G (€450 per day).** This is set below the cost of a senior consultant so the consultancy keeps its margin. It **remains to be validated** with consultancies, because no sector freelance day rate has been verified.
- **Costs the grant does not cover.** Preparation fees cannot be reimbursed from the grant. Under EU grants, an actual cost is eligible only if it is incurred during the action duration set in the grant agreement ([Annotated Grant Agreement v3.0, 1 Nov 2026, Art. 6](https://ec.europa.eu/info/funding-tenders/opportunities/docs/2021-2027/common/guidance/aga_en.pdf)). Proposal preparation happens before that, and a success fee corresponds to no action task. Both are therefore paid from the client's own funds. This is my reading of the rules; confirm it with your NCP for each programme.
