## Key Question 1: Credible 2025–2026 market size and revenue figures, how far sources disagree, and booked revenue vs forecasts

### Takeaway
The two most-cited 2025 figures are McKinsey's "more than $1B" of revenue for quantum computing companies and QED-C's $1.9B for the total market ($1.4B computing, $470M sensing). Both are estimates, not audited sums. Adding up booked 2025 revenue at the companies that are listed today gives only about $250–300M. Most "market" revenue therefore sits with private firms, big-tech quantum units, enabling-hardware suppliers, and government research contracts. The forecasts for 2028 converge on about $3–4.4B for computing. Long-range numbers ($60–100B by 2035) are scenario figures from consultancies with an interest in selling advisory work.

### Inferences
- **Size of the gap.** Listed pure-plays booked roughly $250–300M in 2025, which is 15–30% of the $1–1.4B "quantum computing market." The rest must come from private hardware firms (PsiQuantum, QuEra, Pasqal, Alice & Bob, etc.), big-tech quantum units (IBM, Google, AWS, Microsoft), Chinese vendors, and government R&D contracts. Anyone citing "$1B+ revenue" should know that much of it is not reported anywhere auditable.
- **Range of 2026 market figures.** A 2026 figure of about $1.85–2.5B is plausible, but every top-down figure is an estimate. The QED-C and McKinsey forecasts of 2028 doubling assume that government programmes (DARPA QBI, CHIPS, national strategies) keep paying. That is a demand signal driven by policy, not by end users.
- **Enabling hardware is a real, booked business.** Bluefors' €217M is evidence of this, but the market is concentrated and capital-intensive.
- **PQC is the one layer where estimates already run into the billions.** It is also where the estimates disagree most (4x), which is typical of vendor-research reports. See KQ5.

## Key Question 2: Reported revenue of public pure-plays (2025 and latest 2026 quarters), and their customers

### Takeaway
By November 2026 the listed universe has grown a lot. IonQ, D-Wave, Rigetti, and QCi have been joined by Quantinuum (traditional IPO), IQM, Infleqtion, Xanadu, and Pasqal, each listing in 2026. Only IonQ has revenue at scale, and close to half of its Q3 2026 revenue came from an acquired chip foundry. Every other pure-play books single-digit millions per quarter, posts operating losses 5–25x its revenue, and lives off government contracts, system sales to labs and HPC centres, and capital-markets cash.

## Key Question 3: Private and public funding in 2024, 2025, and 2026 so far, and which budget lines are open to small suppliers

### Takeaway
2025 was a record year for private capital. Totals depend on scope: $3.9B (PitchBook, venture deals), $4.9B (QED-C, all-quantum VC), or $12.6B (McKinsey, including capital-markets deals). In 2026 the money moved to public markets: Quantinuum's $1.7B IPO, IonQ's $2.0B follow-on, and the IQM, Pasqal, Infleqtion, and Xanadu listings. Government money is larger than private money: about $12.7B of new public commitments in 2025, and about $56.7B cumulative announced (QED-C). Most of it goes to a handful of hardware champions through large R&D contracts and equity-for-grant deals. The budget lines most open to small suppliers are procurement-led programmes (UK ProQure, EuroHPC challenges, DOE Genesis Mission proposals) and PQC migration/assurance.

## Key Question 4: Who buys quantum products and services today, what they buy, and typical contract sizes

### Takeaway
Buyers today are mostly publicly funded:
- defence and intelligence agencies (DARPA, AFRL, NASA, UK government);
- national labs and HPC centres (CSC/LUMI, Jülich, RIKEN, EuroHPC, C-DAC);
- universities buying on-prem systems.

A thin layer of large enterprises (banks, pharma, telecoms, chemicals, energy) buys cloud access, co-development, feasibility studies, training, and, increasingly, PQC/security. ## Key Question 6: Expert timelines for fault-tolerant / commercially useful quantum computing, and what they imply for when end-user demand arrives

### Takeaway
Roadmaps converge on **2029** for the first fault-tolerant machines:
- IBM Starling (200 logical qubits, 100M gates)
- Quantinuum Apollo
- Microsoft's goal
- Xanadu (200 logical qubits, 2029–2030)

DARPA's QBI asks whether *any* approach reaches utility scale (value above cost) by **2033**. DARPA's language moved from "unlikely" (2024) to "likely someone" (March 2026). Broad end-user demand for computation therefore realistically arrives around 2029–2033. The 2025–2028 window is about preparation spending: training, use-case discovery, benchmarking, HPC integration, and PQC.

### Cited Findings
- **IBM**: Loon (2025) → Kookaburra (2026, first qLDPC memory module) → Cockatoo (2027, inter-module entanglement) → **Starling (2029), "one hundred million gates on 200 logical qubits"**. Magic-state injection across modules is planned for 2028 — [IBM blog, 10 June 2025](https://www.ibm.com/quantum/blog/large-scale-ftqc).
- **DARPA QBI**
  - Stage C (announced 7 October 2026): Atom Computing, Diraq, IBM, IonQ; Microsoft and PsiQuantum entered earlier via US2QC.
  - Still in Stage B: Quantinuum, QuEra, Photonic, Xanadu.
  - The goal is to determine "whether any quantum-computing approach can achieve utility-scale operation by 2033." The September 2024 briefing called this "unlikely"; in March 2026 DARPA's Micah Stoutimore said someone appeared likely to achieve it.
  - Stage C is not expected to produce the full machine, only enough evidence to finalize a design and start manufacture.
  - Sources: [PostQuantum](https://postquantum.com/industry-news/darpa-quantum-benchmarking-stage-c/); [QCR](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/darpa-selects-atom-computing-diraq-ibm-and-ionq-for-final-stage-c-verification-under-quantum-benchmarking-initiative/).
- **Diraq**: 150,000 physical qubits in 2029, more than 2M on one chip in 2031 — [PostQuantum](https://postquantum.com/industry-news/darpa-quantum-benchmarking-stage-c/).
- **Quantinuum**: Apollo, the fully fault-tolerant machine, "remains on schedule for 2029." Helios has 99.921% two-qubit fidelity — [Andes Qubit](https://andesqubit.com/en/blog/quantum-hardware-roadmaps-2026-2030/) [SECONDARY, via search summary]; [PostQuantum on McKinsey](https://postquantum.com/industry-news/mckinsey-quantum-monitor-2026/).
- **Microsoft**: Majorana 2 prototype (June 2026), a 4-qubit unit cell; goal of a scalable fault-tolerant computer by 2029 — [Andes Qubit](https://andesqubit.com/en/blog/quantum-hardware-roadmaps-2026-2030/) [SECONDARY, via search summary].
- **Xanadu**: commercially viable computer targeted for 2029–2030, with up to 200 logical qubits in 2029, up to 500 the following year, and 1,000+ from 2031 — [Investing.com](https://uk.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/earnings-call-transcript-xanadu-quantum-falls-after-q3-2026-revenue-growth-93CH-4975848).
- **Rigetti**: 300+ qubits at about 99.7% by mid-2028; about 1,000 qubits at about 99.9% roughly three years out. Today it runs a 108-qubit system at 99.5% median two-qubit fidelity — [TQI](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/11/11/rigetti-q3-2026-results-quantum-computing-roadmap/).
- **D-Wave gate model**: 100 logical qubits by 2032 — [D-Wave](https://www.dwavequantum.com/company/newsroom/press-release/d-wave-charts-a-new-course-to-fault-tolerant-quantum-computing-with-gate-model-roadmap/). D-Wave also plans to use CHIPS funding for a 100,000-qubit annealer — [D-Wave 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001907982/000190798226000180/qbts-20261110xexx991.htm).
- **DOE Genesis Q**: up to $215M for a first-generation fault-tolerant, scientifically useful machine by 2028 — [Andes Qubit](https://andesqubit.com/en/blog/quantum-hardware-roadmaps-2026-2030/) [SECONDARY].
- **QuEra survey** [VENDOR-SPONSORED]: 5.6% expect "quantum superiority" within 1–2 years, 37.2% in 3–5 years, 37.5% in 6–10 years, and 16.8% beyond 10 years. 78% consider QEC critical — [QuEra PDF](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/643b94c382e84463a9e52264/6a8ef54f5ca422cd30d018e4_Full_2026_Quantum_Readiness_Survey_Report.pdf).
- **Skeptical timeline view**: Morningstar is cited as putting early commercialization 5–10 years away and general-use quantum computing about 20 years out — [Motley Fool bearish piece, January 2026](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/01/23/predict-quantum-computing-ionq-rigetti-d-wave-buy/) [via search summary; Morningstar original not seen].

### Inferences
- **Before 2029, end-user demand for computation stays exploratory** (cloud experiments, pilots, government-funded on-prem systems).
- **Demand that does not depend on the timeline**: PQC (regulatory deadlines of 2028, 2030, 2031, 2035), workforce training (tied to the QEC talent gap), HPC-quantum integration and benchmarking, and use-case discovery. These run on the vendor/government preparation cycle and are bought now.
- **A downside scenario is real.** If DARPA Stage C or IBM Starling slips, the 2029 consensus would move to the early 2030s and preparation budgets could contract (see KQ7, flat budgets). A capital-light business should not depend on a fault-tolerant hardware date.

### Gaps
- Google's current fault-tolerance roadmap and dates were not retrieved.
- PsiQuantum's current delivery dates (Brisbane, Chicago) were not retrieved.
- Pasqal and Alice & Bob 2026 roadmap updates were not retrieved.
- No independent verification of DARPA Stage B/C results is public (DARPA FAQ restricts disclosure — [PostQuantum](https://postquantum.com/industry-news/darpa-quantum-benchmarking-stage-c/)).

## Key Question 7: The strongest skeptical findings (hype, bubbles, abandoned pilots, low ROI, layoffs, bankruptcies)

### Takeaway
The skeptical evidence is substantial:
- Pure-play stocks fell 41–65% over the year to 10 November 2026.
- Valuations of about 260–1,000x sales.
- Revenue misses at Rigetti and D-Wave, and a D-Wave securities class action.
- IonQ's growth is largely acquired, and its GAAP "profit" is a warrant accounting gain.
- QCi's acquired revenue has a 4.5% gross margin.
- Enterprise surveys show flat budgets, a "show me" phase, and only about 5–13% of respondents in production.

I found no 2026 bankruptcies among quantum pure-plays. They are cash-rich after the 2025–2026 equity raises, which delays any shake-out.

### Cited Findings
- **One-year stock change to 10 November 2026** (and distance below all-time high): IonQ −41.05% (−58.25%); Rigetti −64.11% (−77.68%); D-Wave −64.54% (−75.47%); QCi −50.89% (−98.63%) — [24/7 Wall St. (data: The Information Network)](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/11/10/quantum-computings-next-winners-may-not-be-quantum-stocks/).

Quantinuum's valuation is about 260–285x its 2026 revenue outlook — [Finimize](https://finimize.com/content/qnt-asset-snapshot). D-Wave's price-to-sales ratio is about 300 and Rigetti's about 1,000 or more, per a November 2026 Motley Fool piece [via search summary; exact article not verified].
- **D-Wave class action** (*Fessler v. D-Wave*, N.D. Cal., class period 26 February–25 August 2026)

- **The bubble is in public-market valuations, not in revenue.** Revenue is real but small and mostly government-sourced. That is good for service providers selling into vendors.
- **The risk for a founder** is building on vendor partner programmes or end-user "quantum advantage" projects that stall when budgets stay flat. Revenue tied to compliance deadlines (PQC) or to workforce gaps is more resilient.

## Key Question 8: Structural pain points named repeatedly as unsolved, quantified where possible

### Takeaway
The pain points that keep recurring, with numbers:
- technology immaturity (58%)
- implementation cost (42%)
- unclear ROI (40%)
- talent shortage (37%; fewer than 2,500 QEC specialists; about 3 open roles per qualified candidate)
- hardware access (30%)
- immature algorithms (27%)
- lack of proven pilot results (19%)
- classical/HPC integration (rises from 22% to 31% as organizations mature)
- sovereignty-constrained procurement (62% factor it in)
- uneven capital access (EU 5% of seed funding)
- PQC discovery/inventory obligations with fixed deadlines

### Cited Findings
- **Adoption challenges** (QuEra 2026, n=274 engaged) [VENDOR-SPONSORED]: technology not mature 58.0%; high implementation costs 42.3%; unclear business value/ROI 39.8%; lack of skilled workforce 36.9%; limited hardware access 29.9%; immature algorithms 27.4%; competing budget priorities 22.3%; lack of proven pilot results 19.3%; integration with classical systems 11.3%; lack of executive buy-in 9.1%; regulatory uncertainty 6.9%; security/data privacy 4.4% — [QuEra PDF](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/643b94c382e84463a9e52264/6a8ef54f5ca422cd30d018e4_Full_2026_Quantum_Readiness_Survey_Report.pdf).
- **How challenges change with maturity (QuEra)**, early-stage → advanced-stage organizations:
  - Unclear ROI: 49.2% → 32.5%
  - Workforce gaps: 43.2% → 31.8%
  - Integration challenges: **22.0% → 31.2% (+9.2pp)**
  - The report concludes that "Early-stage organizations need ROI frameworks and talent access; advanced organizations need integration support and hybrid classical-quantum tooling."
  - Source: [QuEra PDF](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/643b94c382e84463a9e52264/6a8ef54f5ca422cd30d018e4_Full_2026_Quantum_Readiness_Survey_Report.pdf).
- **Talent**
  - "the global pool of QEC specialists numbers fewer than 2,500 individuals, with a 3:1 ratio of open quantum positions to qualified candidates." The PhD pipeline takes 5–7 years, so the bottleneck persists "through at least 2030" — [QuEra PDF](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/643b94c382e84463a9e52264/6a8ef54f5ca422cd30d018e4_Full_2026_Quantum_Readiness_Survey_Report.pdf). These are external estimates quoted by QuEra; QCR notes they are not survey data — [QCR](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/quantum-usage-entering-a-show-me-phase-as-c-suite-execs-demand-to-see-measurable-roi/).
  - QED-C: job and internship openings up 11% in 2025; pure-play workforce about 16,500 — [TQI on QED-C](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/04/14/global-quantum-computing-market-to-double-by-2028-reaching-3-billion-in-revenue-qed-c-state-of-the-global-quantum-industry-2026-report-finds/).
- **Organization size (QuEra)**: larger organizations feel *less* prepared (ρ = −0.31), which it attributes to legacy integration, long approval cycles, and competing budgets. It identifies a segment that expects value within 5 years but has low preparedness as "an ideal target for vendors offering accelerated readiness programs" — [QuEra PDF](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/643b94c382e84463a9e52264/6a8ef54f5ca422cd30d018e4_Full_2026_Quantum_Readiness_Survey_Report.pdf).
- **Benchmarking and verification**: DARPA QBI exists to verify claims independently, but proprietary results may not be disclosed. Respondents value logical-qubit demonstrations over raw qubit counts — [PostQuantum](https://postquantum.com/industry-news/darpa-quantum-benchmarking-stage-c/); [QuEra PDF](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/643b94c382e84463a9e52264/6a8ef54f5ca422cd30d018e4_Full_2026_Quantum_Readiness_Survey_Report.pdf).
- **Sovereignty and procurement**: 62% factor sovereignty into procurement; 24% of EU respondents prioritize it — [QCR](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/quantum-usage-entering-a-show-me-phase-as-c-suite-execs-demand-to-see-measurable-roi/). The EU Quantum Act draft reserves procurement for EU-controlled suppliers — [Technology.org](https://www.technology.org/2026/11/14/draft-eu-quantum-act-european-suppliers-logical-qubits-2030/).
- **Capital access**: EU firms get 5% of global pre-seed/seed funding, against more than 50% for US firms — [GovInfoSecurity](https://www.govinfosecurity.com/eu-pushes-quantum-act-to-rival-us-china-a-33478) [via search summary].

### Inferences
- **Pain points a capital-light, AI-leveraged founder could address without hardware**:
  1. **Talent and training** (37% cite it; a 3:1 gap; enterprises such as RBC and Booz Allen already pay vendors for training).
  2. **ROI framing and use-case discovery** for early-stage organizations (49% of early-stage cite unclear ROI).
  3. **PQC cryptographic inventory, discovery, and migration planning** (fixed deadlines, explicit government endorsement of consultancies, services worth 8–12x products).
  4. **Procurement and grant navigation** inside sovereignty-constrained public programmes.
  5. **Vendor-neutral benchmarking and market intelligence** (claims are hard to verify).
- **Pain points that are not addressable without capital and lab access**: hardware maturity, cryogenic supply chain, fabrication.

### Gaps
- No quantified data from non-vendor surveys (e.g., Gartner, Capgemini, or BCG enterprise surveys in 2026) on integration, procurement, or export-control pain points.
- No quantified export-control impact (US/EU/China licensing on cryogenics and quantum items) was found.

## Key Question 9 (cross-cutting, per objective): Evidence on where small, capital-light players earn revenue

### Takeaway
Direct evidence on small players is thin, but the signals point to services rather than products:
- vendors are paid for training (Xanadu → RBC, Booz Allen);
- professional services are the fastest-growing line at D-Wave;
- consultancies are explicitly invited into PQC discovery/assessment (NCSC assurance pilot);
- PQC services are forecast to exceed product revenue by 8–12x;
- 20% of buyers pick vendors partly on "support and professional services," because they cannot build teams internally.

Software and SDK layers are dominated by free open-source tools from well-funded vendors.

### Cited Findings
- Xanadu's first commercial engagements include **training for RBC and Booz Allen** and a feasibility study for JSR — [Investing.com](https://uk.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/earnings-call-transcript-xanadu-quantum-falls-after-q3-2026-revenue-growth-93CH-4975848).
- D-Wave: professional services rose $1.5M in Q3 2026, the largest line increase, while system sales fell. The company also has a services partnership with CGI, an IT services integrator — [D-Wave 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001907982/000190798226000180/qbts-20261110xexx991.htm).
- NCSC: "Consultancies with deep cryptographic expertise can help you understand where you are carrying risk," and an assurance pilot for such consultancies is coming. Smaller organisations on commodity IT will have migration "handled by their vendors" — [NCSC](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/guidance/pqc-migration-timelines).
- PQC consulting/implementation is 38–62% of 2026 PQC spending, and services are forecast at 8–12x products [FORECAST, vendor research] — [MarketsandMarkets](https://www.marketsandmarkets.com/Market-Reports/post-quantum-cryptography-market-126986626.html); [Persistence](https://www.persistencemarketresearch.com/market-research/post-quantum-cryptography-market.asp); [Future Markets](https://www.futuremarketsinc.com/post-quantum-cryptography-market-2026-2036/).
- 20% of survey respondents cite "Support and professional services" as a vendor-selection criterion, which the report attributes to talent scarcity ("Organizations cannot build internal teams and must rely on vendor expertise") — [QuEra PDF](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/643b94c382e84463a9e52264/6a8ef54f5ca422cd30d018e4_Full_2026_Quantum_Readiness_Survey_Report.pdf).
- Mid-size security deals exist: Congruity360's $8.2M PQC + QKD contract with IonQ, and IonQ's acquisition of Elisity (zero-trust) to build out a security platform — [IonQ 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001824920/000119312526449779/ionq-ex99_1.htm).
- Open-source dominance in software: PennyLane is used by about 30% of developers with about 1.1M installs a month, yet commercial software revenue has only "just started" — [Investing.com Xanadu](https://uk.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/earnings-call-transcript-xanadu-quantum-falls-after-q3-2026-revenue-growth-93CH-4975848).
- Well-funded vendors with cash to spend on partners and contractors: IonQ $3.7B pro forma, Quantinuum $1.94B, QCi $1.3B, Infleqtion $560M, D-Wave $503M, Rigetti $497M, Xanadu $286.5M, IQM €260M (KQ2 sources).

### Inferences
- **Most plausible paying layers for a near-zero-capital, AI-agent-leveraged founder, in rough order of booked-demand evidence:**
  1. **PQC readiness services**: cryptographic inventory, discovery reports, migration roadmaps, supplier-questionnaire handling. These are driven by NCSC, OMB, and MAS deadlines, independent of quantum hardware progress, and heavily document and analysis work that AI agents can scale.
  2. **Quantum workforce training and upskilling** for enterprises, governments, and the vendors' own customer-education programmes, given the 3:1 talent gap and that enterprises already pay for it.
  3. **Market intelligence, procurement, and grant/tender support** for the 556 pure-plays and public buyers navigating CHIPS, EuroHPC, ProQure, the EU Quantum Act, and DOE calls.
  4. **Use-case discovery and ROI assessment** for early-stage enterprises.
- **Weakest options for this founder profile**: hardware, enabling components (capital-intensive and concentrated), and standalone quantum algorithm SaaS (competing with free vendor SDKs and few paying users).

### Gaps
- No revenue figures were found for independent quantum consultancies, training providers, or market-intelligence firms (e.g., The Quantum Insider, GQI, Qureca), or for Big-4 quantum or PQC practices. The search budget ran out before these could be researched.
- No pricing benchmarks were found for PQC assessments or quantum training courses.
