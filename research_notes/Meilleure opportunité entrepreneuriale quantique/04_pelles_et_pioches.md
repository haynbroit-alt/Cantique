# "Picks and shovels" around the quantum industry: non-technical and information support businesses (state as of November 2026)

Scope note: this covers 10 support categories (talent, education, market intelligence/media, grants/tenders, export controls/investment screening, IP analytics, standards/benchmarking, procurement/supply chain, investor due diligence) from the viewpoint of a founder with roughly zero capital, no quantum brand, and work done mostly through AI tools and agents. Every claim is dated where the source allows it. The session's web-search budget ran out partway through, so several sub-questions could only be covered by fetching known pages. These are listed under each Gaps section.

Macro context that applies to every category: QED-C data cited by an industry author put private quantum venture investment at $4.9B in 2025, against only $1.4B of total quantum market revenue. PitchBook's narrower count is $3.9B across 127 deals ([PostQuantum, 23 Sept 2026](https://postquantum.com/quantum-commercialization/venture-due-diligence-decline/)). LinkedIn, citing QED-C's *State of the Quantum Industry 2025*, says government funding rose by $3.1B in 2024 to an estimated $44.5B cumulative, and VC reached a record of about $2.6B in 2024 ([LinkedIn Economic Graph, Oct 2025](https://economicgraph.linkedin.com/content/dam/me/economicgraph/en-us/PDF/Quantum-Talent-Trends-in-an-Emerging-Workforce.pdf)). In practice, the customers of any picks-and-shovels business are being paid out of VC and public money, not out of end-customer revenue.

## 1. TALENT / RECRUITING: how severe is the shortage, who serves it, at what fees, and is there room for an AI-agent sourcing business?

### Takeaway
A shortage is widely asserted, but the hard 2025-2026 data shows a small, lumpy and concentrated job market, not a mass shortage. Roughly 9,500 genuine quantum-development job ads were posted worldwide from 2023 to August 2026, from about 1,000 employers led by Google, IBM and AWS. LinkedIn finds that demand for quantum skills peaked in 2022 and that quantum employment has plateaued since 2023, while the pool of people with quantum skills keeps growing. Placement fees are high (20-33% of first-year pay, so about $30-60k per US hire), but several specialist agencies already exist, and the shortage is concentrated in experienced hardware, engineering and commercial roles that require trust and network rather than sourcing volume. An AI-agent sourcing shop could win occasional contingency placements, but its structural odds are poor.

### Cited Findings
**Shortage claims (headline figures)**
- McKinsey states that "there is only one qualified quantum candidate available for every three quantum job openings" and predicted that fewer than 50% of quantum computing jobs would be filled by 2025 without intervention. The figure comes from McKinsey's earlier talent research, and I could not confirm it as a 2025 Monitor figure ([McKinsey, "Five lessons from AI on closing quantum's talent gap"](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/mckinsey-digital/our-insights/five-lessons-from-ai-on-closing-quantums-talent-gap-before-its-too-late)). The World Economic Forum repeated the ratio in November 2025 ([WEF, Nov 2025](https://www.weforum.org/stories/2025/11/upskilling-quantum-talent)).
- An earlier McKinsey Quantum Technology Monitor (2021-era) said university capacity covers only about a third of quantum computing job demand ([McKinsey QTM PDF](https://www.mckinsey.com.br/~/media/mckinsey/business%20functions/mckinsey%20digital/our%20insights/quantum%20computing%20funding%20remains%20strong%20but%20talent%20gap%20raises%20concern/quantum-technology-monitor.pdf)).
- Deloitte projects that 250,000 quantum computing jobs will be needed by 2030 and says the gap between postings and talent is "already significant" ([Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/global/en/insights/topics/emerging-technologies/quantum-computing-futures.html)). The UK Quantum Skills Taskforce cites market analysts' figures of 250,000 new quantum computing jobs globally by 2030 and 840,000 by 2035 ([UK Quantum Skills Taskforce report, DSIT, updated 24 June 2025](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/uk-quantum-skills-taskforce-report/uk-quantum-skills-taskforce-report)).
- QED-C's *State of the Quantum Industry* report found "over 7,000 quantum jobs open globally" (reported June 2025). The QED-C workforce committee lists the main problems as insufficient practical training, unclear career paths, limited affordable and flexible training, and demand for engineers, technicians and "translators" ([The Quantum Insider on the QED-C report, 24 June 2025](https://thequantuminsider.com/2025/06/24/qed-c-report-recommends-ways-to-strengthen-quantum-talent-pipeline-and-fill-critical-quantum-related-positions/)).
- RAND Europe (April 2025) argues that over-reliance on PhD physicists limits workforce growth ([TQI on RAND Europe, 14 Apr 2025](https://thequantuminsider.com/2025/04/14/rand-europe-quantums-future-workforce-needs-more-than-physicists/)). QuEra's 2026 Quantum Readiness Report (a survey of 291 stakeholders in December 2025) lists talent availability among the main adoption obstacles ([IT Brief UK](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/uk-bullish-on-quantum-but-skills-gaps-cloud-outlook)).

**Hard data that tempers the shortage narrative**
- **Coresignal (27 Oct 2026):** Of more than 25,000 ads mentioning "quantum", about 12,000 were quantum-computing related and about 9,500 were "true quantum development roles" between 2023 and August 2026, from about 1,000 distinct employers. Dated postings numbered 239 in 2024, 2,861 in 2025 and 4,518 in January-August 2026. The top advertisers by share of ads were Google (6.41%), IBM (4.37%), AWS (2.98%), Infleqtion (2.74%), IonQ (2.39%), NVIDIA (2.28%), Microsoft (2.26%), Booz Allen (2.00%), **Mercor (1.92%)** and **Mindrift (1.46%)**. The US accounts for more than half of all ads, and software development roles dominate ([Coresignal, 27 Oct 2026](https://coresignal.com/blog/quantum-computing-hiring-2026/)).
- **LinkedIn Economic Graph (Oct 2025):**
  - Global demand for quantum skills peaked in 2022.
  - The share of members employed in quantum occupations has plateaued since 2023, at an index of about 350 versus 100 in January 2016.
  - The wider pool of members with quantum skills or prior quantum jobs keeps rising, up 62% since 2024.
  - The share of quantum-computing job postings rose 74% in 2025 versus 2024 (US +152%, Singapore +85%).
  - 40.6% of members in quantum occupations hold a doctorate and 33.0% a master's degree.
  - More than 25,000 members hold a quantum-focused degree.
  - IBM and Microsoft are the largest employers of people in quantum occupations.
  - The five largest national workforces (US, Germany, UK, India, Canada) account for about two-thirds of the global quantum workforce.
  - 21.6% of members with a core quantum skill also have an AI skill.
  ([LinkedIn, "Quantum talent: Trends in an emerging workforce", Oct 2025](https://economicgraph.linkedin.com/content/dam/me/economicgraph/en-us/PDF/Quantum-Talent-Trends-in-an-Emerging-Workforce.pdf))
- **MIT Quantum Index Report 2026 (published Oct 2026, data to March 2026):** The US share of postings requiring quantum skills rose 16% in 2025 and reached an all-time high in Q1 2026, more than fivefold above 2011 ([MIT QIR 2026 – Workforce](https://qir.mit.edu/workforce)).
- **QED-C monthly monitor:** New quantum job postings fell 20.7% month-on-month globally in November 2025 (Europe −29.5%, North America −24.1%). The trailing 12-month count was roughly flat (+0.3% global). QED-C publishes only percentage changes, not absolute counts ([QED-C Workforce Monitoring Report, 5 Dec 2025](https://quantumconsortium.org/publication/quantum-technology-workforce-monitoring-report-december-5-2025/)).
- **UK:** The UK quantum sector employs only about 1,700 people, at an average salary of about £50,000 (HMRC 2022-23 data). It has at least 160 companies, mostly SMEs with fewer than 50 employees. The report gives no count of unfilled vacancies, but flags hard-to-fill engineering and technician roles and a possible acute shortage of academic technicians. The government has committed to fund 1,000 quantum PhD studentships from 2024 to 2034 ([UK Quantum Skills Taskforce, June 2025](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/uk-quantum-skills-taskforce-report/uk-quantum-skills-taskforce-report)).
- **Australia:** The national strategy projects about 8,700 quantum jobs by 2030 and 19,400 by 2045 ([MIT QIR 2026](https://qir.mit.edu/workforce)).

**Specialist recruiters (named competitors)**
- **DeepRec.ai** (part of Trinnovo Group, a B Corp) has a dedicated quantum division covering hardware, software and sensing, and publishes a 2026 global quantum salary guide. In one case study, a quantum sensing client filled two leadership roles in five weeks after five months of stalled hiring ([DeepRec quantum](https://www.deeprec.ai/disciplines/quantum-computing); [case study](https://www.deeprec.ai/quantum-computing-case-study)).

- **Acceler8 Talent** (Boston/UK) is mainly an AI/ML, semiconductor and photonics recruiter. Quantum appears only as a dropdown option and in a few blog posts ([Acceler8 blog](https://www.acceler8talent.com/resources/blog/ai-recruitment-agency-fees-to-place-a-head-of-ai-in-2026/)).

**Fees (2026)**
- Contingency fees run 15-25% of first-year base salary (US tech median 20%). Specialised roles reach 24-30%, and some boutiques charge a flat 30%.
- Retained search runs 25-33% of first-year total cash (median near 33%), with minimum fees of $80-100k+ paid in thirds. The first third is due whether or not a hire is made.
- Engaged search combines a $5-15k non-refundable retainer with 18-25% at placement.
- Embedded recruiting costs $5-20k per month.
- Fees on roles above $250k base are often capped at $55-65k. Replacement guarantees run 60-90 days.
- Source for all of the above: [Acceler8 Talent, 2026](https://www.acceler8talent.com/resources/blog/ai-recruitment-agency-fees-to-place-a-head-of-ai-in-2026/). Paraform says retained fees run near 33%, citing Korn Ferry and Heidrick filings ([Paraform](https://www.paraform.com/insights/retained-executive-search)).

**Salaries (fee base)**

  - Senior quantum algorithm engineer: $260-350k+ US West; £110-150k UK.
  - Senior quantum hardware engineer: $220-300k+ US West.
  - Mid-level cryogenics engineer: £65-90k UK.
  - Senior technical sales: £90-140k + bonus UK; $160-250k + bonus US West.
  ([DeepRec 2026 salary guide](https://www.deeprec.ai/global-quantum-computing-salary-guide))
- Aggregators come in much lower: Salary.com gives an average quantum physicist base of $118,535 (March 2025) and $98,971 for a quantum computing engineer ([Salary.com](https://www.salary.com/research/salary/hiring/quantum-physicist-salary)). ZipRecruiter gives $101,752 for a quantum engineer (August 2026) ([ZipRecruiter](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Salaries/Quantum-Engineer-Salary)). Glassdoor's "quantum computing" average is $180,678, but its self-reported data is sparse ([Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.com/Salaries/quantum-computing-salary-SRCH_KO0,17.htm)).

**An adjacent "talent" market already captured by AI-native platforms**
- Mercor advertises remote AI-training work for physics and quantum PhDs at $80-110/hr ("Quantum Information Science Physicist (PhD)"), $70-90/hr and $65-85/hr. Screening uses a roughly 20-minute AI interview, and campaigns often last only weeks ([Mercor listing](https://work.mercor.com/m/W39XY6R9); [Mercor physics listing](https://work.mercor.com/jobs/list_AAABnIC9t9Boq6RoblNDtr4i/physics-phd-experts-advanced-quantum-electrodynamics-classical-mechanics); [jobRxiv mirror, $70-90/hr](https://jobrxiv.org/job/physics-phd-experts-string-theory-qft-particle-physics-nuclear-physics/)). Mercor and Mindrift together make up about 3.4% of all quantum job ads ([Coresignal](https://coresignal.com/blog/quantum-computing-hiring-2026/)).

### Inferences
- **Market size is tiny in transactional terms.** About 9,500 true roles over 3.7 years means roughly 2,500-4,500 per year globally. Most are at Google, IBM, AWS, Microsoft and NVIDIA, which source in-house. Startups fill only part of their roles through agencies. A one-person agency would plausibly place 3-10 people a year at $25-50k each, so $75-500k in revenue. That is a living, not a scalable business, and it depends on candidate trust.
- **Who pays:** VC-backed quantum startups with 11-200 staff (LinkedIn finds 51-200 is the modal size) and corporate labs, for senior hardware, control, cryogenics and commercial roles. They do not pay to fill junior software roles, which attract plenty of applicants given that the talent pool is outgrowing employment.
- **Where AI agents help:** cheap long-list building, outreach, and screening for publication records, GitHub and Qiskit contributions. **Where they do not:** convincing a passive senior engineer at Google Quantum AI to move, and earning a hiring manager's trust. Mercor and Paraform-style AI recruiting platforms already compete on agent-driven sourcing.
- **Strongest failure reasons:** hiring is cyclical (QED-C shows sharp monthly drops and LinkedIn shows the 2022 peak); big employers source in-house; established boutiques have networks; candidates are a small, gossipy community where cold AI outreach can damage a brand; no fee is earned until a placement (cash-flow lag of 3-6 months); and the shortage is about experience, which sourcing cannot create.
- **Time to first revenue:** about 3-9 months for a first contingency placement if the founder can find a single client willing to sign terms. It is faster only with an existing network.

### Gaps
- I found no published fee schedule for any quantum-specific recruiter (DeepRec, Quantum Futures, Darwin), and no revenue or headcount for Quantum Futures. QuantumJobs.us, Quantum Computing Jobs and the Qureca/IQM boards were not checked: their revenue models and listing counts are unknown.
- A figure saying 61% of QED-C members cited "lack of qualified candidates" as the top hiring barrier (early-2024 survey) appeared only in a search snippet. I could not open and verify the primary page.
- McKinsey's 2025 Quantum Technology Monitor talent section could not be retrieved (HTTP 503). The Sifted article from May 2026 on the commercial talent crunch at European quantum startups was blocked (HTTP 403).

## 2. EDUCATION / TRAINING / UPSKILLING: who sells it, at what price, who profits, and is executive or professional "quantum readiness" a paid market?

### Takeaway
Quantum education is abundantly supplied, heavily subsidised and largely free at the entry level. Q-CTRL Black Opal has a free tier, Womanium is a nonprofit, and programmes from IBM and governments are free or publicly funded. Paid price points exist: MIT xPRO charges $2,500 for a two-course programme, QURECA charges $200-1,000 per course and $3,000 per learning path, and Black Opal Pro was about $120-200 lifetime. Corporate and executive training is sold on quote. I found no evidence of a profitable standalone quantum-education company or a notable 2025-2026 acquisition. The main documented deal is The Quantum Insider buying Q-munity in 2024. Non-English and profession-specific executive briefings (lawyers, bankers, CISOs on post-quantum cryptography) are the only niches with plausible willingness to pay, and that is inferred, not evidenced.

### Cited Findings
- **Q-CTRL Black Opal:**
  - The basic version is free.
  - "Black Opal Pro" lifetime access was listed at USD 199.99 and discounted to 119.99 in a 2025 Black Friday offer that expired 12 December 2025 ([Q-CTRL Black Friday page](https://q-ctrl.com/lp/black-opal/black-friday-sale)). A 2022 article quoted $9.99/month ([Optica OPN, Oct 2022](https://www.optica-opn.org/home/industry/2022/october/q-ctrl_targets_enterprise-scale_quantum_training)).
  - Enterprise and educator plans are quote-only. The enterprise tier offers LMS deployment, course customisation and cohort analytics ([Q-CTRL pricing page](https://www.q-ctrl.com/black-opal/pricing); [AWS Marketplace listing](https://aws.amazon.com/marketplace/pp/prodview-nqmmgddo42tuk)).
  - Licences were given away fully subsidised to Australian TAFE students and HBCU institutions ([SmartCompany](https://www.smartcompany.com.au/technology/tafe-q-ctrl-partner-australia-quantum-education-landscape/); [QCR](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/q-ctrl-and-the-quad-investors-network-will-provide-free-black-opal-licenses-to-students-at-certain-educational-institutions-in-australia-and-the-u-s/)).
- **QURECA (UK/Spain):**
  - Course prices: "Quantum for Everyone 2.0" (15 h) at 400, shown at £155 on sale; "Quantum Strategy for Business" (4 h) at 200, shown at £78 on sale; Quantum Sensing (30 h) at 600; Quantum Machine Learning at 1,000; Quantum Security at €800.
  - Learning paths: $3,000 per person for courses 1-5; $2,500 for the computing and communication combination.
  - Corporate training is customised and quoted.
  - Source pages: [QURECA learning paths](https://academy.qureca.com/learning-paths/); [Quantum Training for Business](https://academy.qureca.com/product/quantum-training-for-business/).
  - QURECA and QuantrolOx launched a hands-on "Quantum EDGE Academy" ([Quantum Zeitgeist](https://quantumzeitgeist.com/?p=348545)).
  - Currencies are mixed across storefronts.
- **MIT xPRO:** "Quantum Computing Fundamentals" costs $2,500 for two four-week courses ($1,600 per course), with no scholarships or financial aid. "Quantum Computing: Strategy and Impact" costs $1,500. The programmes are aimed at business professionals and leaders ([MIT xPRO QCF](https://xpro.mit.edu/programs/program-v1:xPRO+QCF/); [MIT support article](https://support.learn.mit.edu/hc/en-us/articles/42231693811227-What-is-the-cost-of-Quantum-Computing-Algorithms-for-Cybersecurity-Chemistry-and-Optimization); [Strategy & Impact](https://prolearn.mit.edu/quantum-computing-strategy-and-impact)).
- **qBraid:**
  - Total raised is about $350K, mostly grants, per CB Insights ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/qbraid)), or $1.67M per Caplight ([Caplight](https://www.caplight.com/company/qbraid)).
  - It received a $300K NSF POSE Phase I grant ([QCR](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/qbraid-secures-300k-nsf-grant-to-advance-hardware-agnostic-sdk-for-quantum-software/)).
  - It runs qBook courses and hosted IBM's Qiskit Global Summer School ([qBraid about](https://www.qbraid.com/about)).
  - No revenue is disclosed.
- **Womanium:** A not-for-profit in Washington, DC. Earlier editions drew 4,000+ students (45% women) from 100+ countries, and the programme is open to all with scholarships ([Koç QuEST](https://quest.ku.edu.tr/?p=1218); [TQI 2024](https://thequantuminsider.com/2024/06/03/womanium-launches-global-quantumai-program-2024/)). An aggregator says WISER's 2026 summer programme had 1,500+ participants from 33 countries (unverified) ([Superscout](https://superscout.co/program/womanium-wiser-quantum-program-2025)).
- **Consolidation:** The Quantum Insider (a Resonance subsidiary) acquired Q-munity, a quantum education and workforce-development organisation, in May 2024 ([TQI, 2 May 2024](https://thequantuminsider.com/2024/05/02/accelerating-quantum-education-resonance-subsidiary-the-quantum-insider-invests-in-q-munity-leader-in-workforce-development/)). D-Wave's January 2026 acquisition of Quantum Circuits will initially be "focused more on research and education", but it is a hardware deal ([Washington Times, 8 Jan 2026](https://m.washingtontimes.com/news/2026/jan/8/d-wave-quantum-computing-company-acquires-new-tech-major-merger/)).
- **Public programmes competing with private sellers:**
  - EU: a pilot Quantum Apprenticeship Programme and a European Quantum Academy (2026).
  - UK: increased quantum apprenticeship funding (2026) and a Centre for Quantum Commercialisation Skills competition.
  - US: an Executive Order (June 2026) creating National QIST Workforce Development Institutes, and NSF ExpandQISE ($39M, 2024).
  - Source: [MIT QIR 2026](https://qir.mit.edu/workforce).
  - The UK taskforce recommends DSIT-mapped skills, apprenticeships for five high-demand roles, and short quantum modules for apprentices ([UK Quantum Skills Taskforce](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/uk-quantum-skills-taskforce-report/uk-quantum-skills-taskforce-report)).
- QED-C itself names the "lack of affordable, flexible training" as a gap ([TQI on QED-C, June 2025](https://thequantuminsider.com/2025/06/24/qed-c-report-recommends-ways-to-strengthen-quantum-talent-pipeline-and-fill-critical-quantum-related-positions/)).

### Inferences
- **Who pays today:** individuals pay $100-3,000 one-off. Corporates and governments buy enterprise seats and custom workshops on quote, and universities buy educator licences that are often subsidised. MIT's $1,500-2,500 programmes set the price anchor for executives.
- **Gap:** role-specific "quantum readiness" for non-physicists is under-supplied in paid, credible form. Examples: post-quantum cryptography migration for CISOs and banks, export-control awareness for university tech-transfer offices, and quantum literacy for lawyers and investors. So is anything in languages other than English, though that is inferred from the absence of results, not from a search.
- **Defensibility is low.** Content is easily replicated by AI, big incumbents give it away (Black Opal free tier, IBM), and buyers want brand credentials such as MIT or Q-CTRL.
- **Time to first revenue:** about 1-3 months for a paid workshop or cohort if the founder has a channel, such as a chamber of commerce, a bar association or a CISO community. Without a channel, expect a long organic-audience build.
- **Strongest failure reasons:** free substitutes, low and one-off willingness to pay, no credential, the risk of teaching errors in a technical field, and demand driven by hype that can evaporate.

### Gaps
- I did not verify prices or revenue for Classiq's academy, Quantum Rise, QuantumGrad, The Coding School / Qubit by Qubit (search returned nothing), Brilliant, Coursera or edX quantum courses, or IBM Quantum Learning (believed to be free, but not verified in this session).
- I found no evidence on the size of the corporate executive quantum-training market or on non-English offers.

## 3. MARKET INTELLIGENCE / DATA / MEDIA: incumbents, prices, business models, and missing data products

### Takeaway
The space is already occupied by a funded consolidator, The Quantum Insider (TQI), owned by Resonance. TQI has 11-50 staff, 200+ enterprise and government clients, $2,999-5,999 datasets, a per-seat platform subscription, advisory work, marketing services and a supplier marketplace. Other players are GQI (which owns the Quantum Computing Report), IQT Research, IDTechEx, ad-funded Quantum Zeitgeist, and strong free public sources such as the MIT Quantum Index Report and EPO/OECD studies. A zero-capital entrant cannot out-cover TQI. The only openings are narrow, verifiable data products TQI does not obviously sell, such as structured tender and grant feeds, cloud QPU pricing trackers or claims-verification scorecards, and even these have few paying buyers.

### Cited Findings
- **TQI / Resonance:**
  - Founded 2018, 11-50 employees, part of Resonance Alliance Inc. (Toronto).
  - More than 200 enterprise clients.
  - Business lines: news, the TQI Intelligence Platform, advisory, and marketing services (content, SEO, campaigns for quantum companies).
  - Source: [TQI About](https://thequantuminsider.com/about/).
  - Standalone datasets (an Excel table plus a PDF briefing) cost **$2,999 for a single licence or $5,999 for a corporate licence**. The Intelligence Platform is an annual per-seat subscription with an undisclosed price. All market reports are bundled into the platform, and advisory work is custom-scoped (page dated September-November 2026) ([TQI "dataset or platform"](https://thequantuminsider.com/datasets/guides/dataset-or-platform-which-do-i-need/)).
  - TQI claims 2,100+ quantum companies tracked and 200+ enterprise and government clients as of Q4 2026 ([TQI due-diligence page](https://thequantuminsider.com/quantum-computing-consulting/due-diligence/)).
  - Resonance launched in 2022 to extend the model to adjacent frontier technologies ([TQI, 20 Dec 2022](https://thequantuminsider.com/2022/12/20/the-quantum-insider-launches-resonance-as-it-expands-market-intelligence-offering-to-adjacent-frontier-technologies/)). CEO Alex Challans. Resonance joined a $25M New Mexico quantum initiative (August 2025) ([TQI, 25 Aug 2025](https://thequantuminsider.com/2025/08/25/resonance-to-support-25-million-new-mexico-quantum-initiative/)).
  - No funding or revenue figures were found.
- **GQI (Global Quantum Intelligence):** Formed in 2022 as a dedicated market and business intelligence firm. It owns the Quantum Computing Report (QCR) and sells an annual membership to its portal. Prices are not public, and outlooks are members-only ([QCR announcement](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/global-quantum-intelligence-gqi-formed-as-a-dedicated-market-and-business-intelligence-firm-focused-on-quantum-tech/); [QCR 2025 mid-year outlook](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/global-quantum-ecosystem-analysis-gqis-2025-mid-year-outlook/amp/)).
- **IQT Research (Inside Quantum Technology):** Publishes an annual Quantum Technology Industry Report each April since 2023 with an eight-year forecast, plus QKD, sensor, PQC and QRNG studies. They are resold through MarketResearch.com and GII. No prices were retrieved ([MarketResearch.com listing](https://marketresearch.com/Inside-Quantum-Technology-Research-v4306/Quantum-Technology-38776840); [GII](https://giiresearch.com/report/iqt1361819-quantum-technology-industry-report.html)).
- **IDTechEx** sells a "Quantum Technology Market 2024-2034" report ([IDTechEx](https://www.idtechex.com/QuantumTech)). Research and Markets sells a quantum computing supply-chain report covering 2026-2036 ([GlobeNewswire, 12 May 2026](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/05/12/3292701/0/en/Quantum-Computing-Supply-Chain-Research-Report-2026-2036-Materials-Components-and-Enabling-Hardware-Critical-to-Quantum-Computing-Across-All-Major-Qubit-Modalities.html)).
- **Quantum Zeitgeist:**
  - Owned by Hadamard LLC (Wyoming), with a "small" team of six named staff.
  - Funded by advertising and commercial partnerships, and also sells media, PR and consultancy services. Articles are free.
  - Claims 18,000+ articles and readers in 80+ countries. Its "Navigator" directory lists 1,597 companies in 62 countries (October 2026).
  - Source: [Quantum Zeitgeist About](https://quantumzeitgeist.com/about/).
  - **This is the closest documented example of a small, bootstrapped quantum media operation.**
- **Newsletters:** There are Substack and LinkedIn quantum newsletters ([quantumcomputing.substack.com](https://quantumcomputing.substack.com/about); [André König, "Quantum World Detangled"](https://reletter.com/publications/quantum-world-detangled-andre-m-konig)). No subscriber or revenue figures were found.
- **Free public intelligence that undercuts paid products:**
  - The MIT Quantum Index Report (2025 and 2026 editions) covers workforce, patents and investment ([MIT QIR](https://qir.mit.edu/workforce)).
  - The EPO/OECD study "Mapping the global quantum ecosystem" (December 2025) includes a patent landscape ([EPO PDF](https://link.epo.org/web/publications/studies/en-mapping-the-global-quantum-ecosystem.pdf)).
  - QED-C publishes its monthly job-postings monitor ([QED-C](https://quantumconsortium.org/publication/quantum-technology-workforce-monitoring-report-december-5-2025/)).

This suggests the small independent analyst operation lapsed (fetched 22 Nov 2026) ([factbasedinsight.com](https://www.factbasedinsight.com/)).

### Inferences
- **Who pays:** corporate strategy teams, governments and regional quantum clusters (TQI's named clients include UK DSIT/BIST, MassTech, North Carolina and Chattanooga), investors, and quantum vendors buying marketing. Ad revenue is small because the audience is niche.
- **Data products that do not appear to exist as standalone, priced products** (inferred; the closest incumbents are noted):
  - A structured, multilingual feed of quantum tenders and calls (EU Funding & Tenders, TED, EuroHPC, Chips JU, SAM.gov, SBIR, UKRI, national agencies) with deadlines and consortium matching. TQI tracks "programs", but no tender alerting product was found.
  - A tracker of public cloud QPU-time prices across IBM, AWS Braket, Azure, IonQ and others.
  - A claims-verification log that compares vendor roadmaps and SPAC decks with delivered results. PostQuantum does this ad hoc.
  - An export-control classification lookup for quantum components.
  - Supplier lead-time indices.
- **AI fit:** each of these can be built by an AI agent from public sources. That is also why defensibility is weak, since TQI or GQI could copy any that gains traction.
- **Strongest failure reasons:** TQI already bundles most data and reports into one subscription; free MIT, OECD and QED-C sources exist; the buyer universe is perhaps hundreds of organisations; and credibility requires a brand built over years. A 1-2 person media site survives on ads and sponsored content (Zeitgeist), which creates conflicts of interest and a low ceiling.
- **Time to first revenue:** a niche dataset or tender feed sold at €50-500 per month to quantum SMEs could land a first customer in 1-3 months, if it saves visible time. Selling to corporates is a 6-12 month cycle.

### Gaps
- Not covered: Hyperion Research's quantum practice and prices; GQI membership prices (the subscribe page returned 404); IQT and IDTechEx report prices; TQI platform seat price and revenue; Olivier Ezratty's *Understanding Quantum Technologies* (the page returned HTTP 429), believed to be a free book; Quantum Pirates; the Quantum Economic Network; and QuEra's newsletter.

## 4. FUNDING / GRANT ADVISORY AND TENDER INTELLIGENCE: how much public money flows, and is quantum-specialised grant writing or tender monitoring a paid market?

### Takeaway
Public money is large and growing, which makes this the strongest objective "pull" among the information businesses. Documented examples include $2.0B in US CHIPS quantum incentive letters of intent, the US DOE's $625M centre renewal (November 2025), the EU's Quantum Europe Strategy with six chip pilot lines and a 2026 Quantum Act, EuroHPC's six new quantum calls (August 2026), and the UK's £1bn+ programme. Cumulative government quantum funding is about $44.5B. Grant consulting is a proven business model (success fee plus retainer), but EU rules discipline it, free National Contact Points compete with it, and well-funded AI grant tools ($55M for Instrumentl) are commoditising the writing itself. No quantum-specialised grant consultancy or tender feed was identified, which is a real gap, though the client base is small (hundreds of quantum SMEs and labs per region).

### Cited Findings
**Money volumes**
- **Global:** Government funding rose by $3.1B in 2024 to an estimated $44.5B cumulative (QED-C via [LinkedIn, Oct 2025](https://economicgraph.linkedin.com/content/dam/me/economicgraph/en-us/PDF/Quantum-Talent-Trends-in-an-Emerging-Workforce.pdf)).
- **US:**
  - The Commerce Department signed nine letters of intent for **$2.013B** of CHIPS Act quantum incentives, including $1B for an IBM quantum foundry, with provisions for government equity stakes (2026) ([PYMNTS, 2026](https://www.pymnts.com/technology/2026/us-government-invests-2-billion-in-quantum-computing-leaders/)).
  - DOE committed **$625M** to renew its five National QIS Research Centers (4 November 2025) ([DOE](https://www.energy.gov/articles/energy-department-announces-625-million-advance-next-phase-national-quantum-information)).
  - DARPA awarded **$125M** to PsiQuantum ([Network World](https://www.networkworld.com/article/4200767/federal-quantum-bet-grows-with-darpas-125-million-psiquantum-award.html)).
  - NSF X-Labs plans $1.5B over ten years, including quantum sensors. Baseline federal QIS spending is about $1B a year, and a $2.7B NQI reauthorization was still pending ([University of South Carolina landscape PDF 2025](https://www.sc.edu/about/offices_and_divisions/provost/docs/emerging-technology-series-quantum-information-science-landscape-2025.pdf); [Cointelegraph on the $6B science push](https://cointelegraph.com/news/trump-administration-quantum-computing-ai-6-billion-science-push)).
- **EU:**
  - The Quantum Europe Strategy (2 July 2025) plans six quantum chip pilot lines plus a design facility and a quantum internet pilot, with the Quantum Act scheduled for 2026 ([European Commission, 2 July 2025](https://commission.europa.eu/news-and-media/news/eus-plan-become-global-leader-quantum-2030-2025-07-02_en)).
  - A secondary source cites up to €50M of public funding for the pilot lines ([ERA Austria, 3 July 2025](https://era.gv.at/news-items/new-quantum-europe-strategy-aims-to-transform-europes-scientific-excellence-into-economic-success/)).
  - EuroHPC JU launched six new quantum calls (14 August 2026) and runs calls for quantum experimental pilot lines ([EuroHPC](https://www.eurohpc-ju.europa.eu/six-new-quantum-calls-launched-eurohpc-joint-undertaking-2026-08-14_en)).
  - Industry position papers on the Quantum Act were still being filed in October 2026 ([SEMI position paper](https://www.semi.org/sites/semi.org/files/2026-10/Position_Paper-European_Quantum_Act.pdf)).
- **UK:** The National Quantum Technologies Programme invested about £1bn from 2014 to 2023 ([UK Quantum Skills Taskforce](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/uk-quantum-skills-taskforce-report/uk-quantum-skills-taskforce-report)).
- **Private (for comparison):** $4.9B of venture investment in 2025 per QED-C, or $3.9B across 127 deals per PitchBook ([PostQuantum](https://postquantum.com/quantum-commercialization/venture-due-diligence-decline/)).

**The grant-consulting market and its rules**
- The EIC Board says applicants may use consultants but "success in EIC calls is achievable without them". It warns about exclusivity clauses, IP terms and "the nature of success fees", recommends comparing several offers (the code says to request at least three) and an NDA, and points to free alternatives: National Contact Points, technology transfer offices and EIC coaching ([EIC Board observations, 28 Feb 2025](https://eic.ec.europa.eu/news/eic-board-observations-use-consultants-eic-applications-2025-02-28_en)).

- RVO puts the average EIC Accelerator success rate at about 7% and lists academic-research-based deep tech as a "green flag" profile ([RVO Red and Green Flags V2, 2025](https://www.rvo.nl/sites/default/files/2025-05/Red-and-Green-Flags-EIC-Accelerator-V2.pdf)).
- Under Horizon Europe the grant generally cannot reimburse the writer's fee, so the fee comes from the applicant's own budget (a secondary blog, not official guidance) ([Grantbite](https://www.grantbite.com/en/blog/grant-writing-services)).

**AI-native grant tools**
- **Instrumentl** raised **$55M** led by Summit Partners in April 2025 for its AI grant discovery, writing and management platform (US, mostly nonprofits) ([Silicon UK / Business Wire](https://www.silicon.co.uk/press-release/instrumentl-raises-55m-from-summit-partners-to-accelerate-their-ai-grant-fundraising-platform)).
- **Grantable** has raised about $100K per CB Insights ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/grantable/)). Its prices are Free / $50 / $150 per month, plus a $300/month "Agency Hub" for consultants ([Grantable pricing](https://grantable.co/pricing)). It claims "30,000+ grant professionals" ([Grantable LP](https://grantable.co/lp/ai-grant-writer)).
- **Granter** (Portugal) raised €1.6M, for €2M total, to take AI grant writing across Europe ([Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/169102-portugal-s-granter-raises-1-6m-to-take-ai-grant-writing-tool-across-euro/)).
- Grant Assistant was covered by TechCrunch in November 2023 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/11/01/grant-assistant-wants-to-apply-generative-ai-to-grant-proposals)).

### Inferences
- **Who pays today:** EU, UK and US deep-tech SMEs and spin-outs pay grant consultants a retainer plus a success fee (percentages unverified, see Gaps). Large firms have in-house teams. Universities use internal grant offices.
- **Gap:** I found no quantum-specialised grant consultancy or tender-intelligence feed. A combined "quantum calls radar plus consortium matching plus AI-drafted first versions reviewed by the founder" service is plausible for EU and UK quantum SMEs, especially with the 2026-2027 Quantum Act, Chips JU and EuroHPC calls.
- **AI fit:** high for monitoring, eligibility triage, drafting and compliance checklists. Lower for the technical excellence sections, which need the client's scientists.
- **Defensibility:** moderate. Value comes from relationships, a track record of wins and consortium networks that build with time. Pure writing is being commoditised by Instrumentl and Grantable-type tools at $50-150/month.
- **Time to first revenue:** the fastest of all categories. A paid tender-alert subscription or a fixed-fee proposal review (around €1-5k) could close within 2-8 weeks of contact. Success fees arrive only months later, after evaluation (EIC decisions take several months), so cash flow is lagged.
- **Strongest failure reasons:**
  - Success fees on calls with roughly 7% success rates mean much unpaid work.
  - EIC guidance discourages applicants from consultants, and free NCPs compete.
  - The founder has no track record to show, which the code of conduct requires disclosing.
  - Call calendars are seasonal.
  - Quantum is a small slice of deep tech, so a quantum-only focus may be too narrow, while a general deep-tech focus is crowded.

### Gaps
- I found no verified figures for typical EU or UK grant-consultant success fees (often quoted informally at 5-15%) or retainers. I found no SBIR/STTR quantum award totals, no Innovate UK, India NQM, Japan or Korea programme figures, no EIC quantum-specific award counts, and no revenue or usage figures for AI grant tools in Europe. The search budget ran out before these could be covered.

## 5. EXPORT CONTROLS / INVESTMENT SCREENING / REGULATORY COMPLIANCE: what rules apply, who pays for help, what is unserved?

### Takeaway
The rulebook thickened substantially in 2024-2026:
- the US BIS interim final rule (September 2024), with 18 new ECCNs, License Exception IEC and a deemed-export carve-out with reporting duties;
- quantum additions to the US Entity List in March and September 2025;
- the US outbound investment rule, in force since 2 January 2025, which prohibits or requires notification of quantum investments in China;
- the COINS Act (December 2025), whose proposed rule (22 October 2026) adds quantum algorithms and applications and more countries, with comments due 7 December 2026 and a final rule due by 13 March 2027;
- national controls in Germany, France, the UK, Spain, the Netherlands and Canada (2024);
- EU Delegated Regulation 2025/2003, in force 15 November 2025, adding quantum computers and cryogenic items to the EU-wide list.

Demand for compliance help is real, but today it is served by large law firms and trade-compliance software. Their target market is less the cash-poor startups and university labs, which suggests an under-served segment. The barrier is liability: classification and licensing advice is legal advice.

### Cited Findings
- **US BIS IFR (published 6 September 2024):**
  - Adds 18 ECCNs and revises 9, covering quantum computers and related equipment, components, materials, software and technology.
  - Creates License Exception IEC for countries with equivalent controls.
  - Deemed exports and reexports are "largely exempt", except for nationals of countries such as China and Russia or arms-embargoed countries. Exporters must meet annual reporting requirements.
  - Licences are needed for certain allied destinations from 5 November 2024.
  - Source: [Orrick, Sept 2024](https://orrick.com/Insights/2024/09/US-Joins-Allies-to-Impose-Export-Controls-on-Quantum-Computing-and-Other-Emerging-Technologies). BIS invited comments on deemed-export policy under docket BIS-2024-0020 ([BIS press release](https://www.bis.gov/press-release/department-commerce-implements-controls-quantum-computing-other-advanced-technologies-alongside); [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2024/09/us-commerce-implements-export-controls-on-advanced-technology-in-concert-with-international-partners)).
- **Entity List 2025:** The March 2025 package added 80 entities (54 in China), seven of them for helping China develop quantum computing ([The Register, 26 Mar 2025](https://www.theregister.com/on-prem/2025/03/26/dozens-of-banned-chinese-offshoots-added-to-us-entity-list/427788)). The September 2025 rule added 32 entities (23 in China), including the Chinese Academy of Sciences National Time Service Center, citing quantum capabilities ([Federal Register 2025-17893, 16 Sept 2025](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-09-16/html/2025-17893.htm)).
- **US outbound investment (OISP):** Effective 2 January 2025. It prohibits US-person transactions related to developing quantum computers, certain quantum networks and sensing platforms in China, and requires notification of others ([AIP FYI](https://www.aip.org/fyi/us-treasury-restricts-investment-in-chinese-quantum-industry); [Treasury final rule](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/206/TreasuryDepartmentOutboundInvestmentFinalRuleWEBSITEVERSION.pdf)).
- **COINS Act and NPRM:**
  - The COINS Act was enacted 18 December 2025 in the FY2026 NDAA. Treasury's NPRM followed on 22 October 2026.
  - The NPRM keeps the quantum prohibitions and adds "algorithms and applications for quantum information science" (with an end-use limit).
  - It adds Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Russia and Venezuela-linked entities as countries of concern.
  - Comments are due 7 December 2026, and the final rule is due by 13 March 2027.
  - It sets a $50k de minimis for notifications (not prohibitions) and exempts LPs committing under $2M.
  - Investors would need diligence on the target's activities, ownership, control and affiliates, and restricted-party screening of whole corporate families.
  - Treasury imposed a $200k penalty on Amidi LLC in July 2026 for failing to notify a roughly $92k investment.
  - Source: [Kirkland & Ellis alert, Nov 2026](https://www.kirkland.com/publications/kirkland-alert/2026/11/treasury-poised-to-significantly-expand-us); also [Orrick, Nov 2026](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/11/Treasury-Proposes-Expanded-Outbound-Investment-Rules).
- **National controls (2024):** Canada, Germany, France, the UK, Spain and the Netherlands issued national quantum export restrictions between February and July 2024, outside Wassenaar consensus. Germany's text covers quantum computers with low gate errors and more than 34 qubits ([DFN, 31 July 2024](https://win-labor.dfn.de/en/2024/07/31/export-restrictions-quantum-computers)).
- **EU:**
  - The Commission adopted the update on 8 September 2025. Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/2003 was published 14 November 2025 and took effect 15 November 2025.
  - It adds quantum computers, cryogenic-temperature electronics, parametric signal amplifiers, cryogenic cooling systems and cryogenic wafer probers. A Commission presentation places quantum computers under entry 4A506, with qubit-count and CNOT-error parameters.
  - The Netherlands updated its national measures on 24 November 2025, retroactively from 15 November.
  - Companies must reclassify products, check licences and update internal controls, including for intangible transfers such as remote and cloud access and R&D collaboration.
  - Sources: [Commission, 8 Sept 2025](https://policy.trade.ec.europa.eu/news/2025-update-eu-control-list-dual-use-items-2025-09-08_en); [Cooley, 5 Dec 2025](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2025/2025-12-05-eu--issues-2025-update-to-dual-use-control-list); [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/eu-updates-dualuse-control-list-new-controls-on-emerging-technologies-and-shift-in-export-control); [Baker McKenzie](https://sanctionsnews.bakermckenzie.com/eu-commission-adopts-delegated-regulation-updating-annex-i-of-the-eu-dual-use-regulation/).
- **China's counter-leverage:** Chinese export controls now cover dual-use items containing 0.1% or more of Chinese-origin rare-earth value. Quantum depends on erbium, ytterbium and neodymium for lasers and amplifiers, and on rubidium and strontium for neutral atoms. China holds about 90% of rare-earth processing ([War on the Rocks, Oct 2025](https://warontherocks.com/2025/10/the-supply-chain-chokepoints-in-quantum/)).
- **Who serves today:** large law firms publishing client alerts and selling counsel (Orrick, MoFo, Mayer Brown, Dorsey, Akin, Hogan Lovells, Baker McKenzie, Cooley, Kirkland, CMS, Cuatrecasas), trade-compliance content providers such as Thomson Reuters ONESOURCE Global Trade ([TR, Sept 2024](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en-us/help/onesource-global-trade/regulatory-insights/2024/september-10th/us-new-controls-quantum-computing.html)), and WSGR's quantum industry practice ([WSGR](https://www.wsgr.com/en/services/industries/quantum-computing.html)). TQI's QHub lets buyers filter vendors "by export rules and agreements" ([TQI QHub article, Nov 2025](https://thequantuminsider.com/2025/11/11/everything-you-need-to-know-about-the-quantum-supply-chain-hub-qhub/)).

### Inferences
- **Who would pay:**
  - Quantum hardware and component SMEs that export (cryo, control electronics, lasers) and now need EU 2025/2003 and national classification.
  - VC funds and family offices that must run COINS/OISP diligence, with a strong trigger from the final rule due by March 2027.
  - University tech-transfer and research-security offices handling deemed exports and foreign researchers.
- **The unserved gap** is likely affordable, productised tooling rather than legal opinions. Examples:
  - an AI-assisted pre-classification questionnaire mapping products to 4A506-type entries and US ECCNs;
  - deemed-export record-keeping templates for labs;
  - an OISP/COINS "quantum activity screening" checklist and data room questionnaire for investors;
  - a multi-jurisdiction rules tracker (US, EU, UK, DE, FR, ES, NL, JP, CA) in plain language.
- **AI fit:** high for monitoring and drafting. However, the outputs are compliance determinations, so liability and unauthorised practice of law are real constraints. The likely model is "software plus partner law firm" or selling to compliance officers, not determinations.
- **Defensibility:** moderate. Regulatory content is public, but a maintained, structured, multi-jurisdiction quantum control database with change alerts compounds over time. Incumbents (Thomson Reuters, Descartes-type tools, law firms) are generalist.
- **Time to first revenue:** about 1-3 months for a paid briefing or checklist sold to a VC fund or a cluster association, timed to the COINS comment window (December 2026) and final rule (March 2027). Recurring SaaS revenue is slower.
- **Strongest failure reasons:**
  - Startups often ignore compliance until a deal or shipment forces it, and then hire a lawyer.
  - Universities have in-house export-control officers.
  - Errors carry legal exposure.
  - The volume of quantum exports is still small.
  - Big-law alerts are free marketing.

### Gaps
- Not verified: Japan's quantum export controls (2024-2025), China's specific quantum counter-controls beyond rare earths, the UK's 2024 SI details, any BIS 2025-2026 amendment specific to quantum ECCNs, and the status of the September 2025 BIS "affiliates" (50%) rule. I found no evidence of a compliance vendor specialised in quantum, and no prices for export-control classification work.

## 6. IP / PATENT ANALYTICS and STANDARDS / BENCHMARKING / CERTIFICATION: is anyone paying, and who provides it?

### Takeaway
Quantum patent data is abundant and largely free at the landscape level: MIT QIR, EPO/OECD (December 2025) and academic studies. Paid IP work sits with law firms and with incumbent analytics suites, including TQI's platform, which has patent data. Independent benchmarking has one notable exit, Keysight's 2021 acquisition of Quantum Benchmark (Waterloo, error diagnostics and performance validation). Today community benchmarks (Unitary Foundation's Metriq) are free, and there is "no accepted progress metric" in quantum. Standards tracking (ISO/IEC JTC 3, IEEE, ETSI, CEN-CENELEC) could not be verified in this session. For a zero-capital founder, IP analytics and certification look like "false good ideas" as standalone businesses. They are better as features of due-diligence or intelligence products.

### Cited Findings
- **Patent volumes:** Quantum patent filing grew fivefold from 2014 to 2024. China filed 7,308 quantum patents in 2024, up from 1,011 in 2014, and held about 60% of all quantum technology patents as of 2024 ([Foley, Oct 2025](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2025/10/pivotal-push-from-lab-to-leverage-why-now-is-the-window-to-secure-foundational-quantum-patents/); [MIT QIR – Patents](https://qir.mit.edu/patents)).
- **Free or official landscapes:** the EPO and OECD "Mapping the global quantum ecosystem" (December 2025) ([EPO](https://link.epo.org/web/publications/studies/en-mapping-the-global-quantum-ecosystem.pdf)), and Aboy, Crespo and Minssen's 2025 landscape update with data to October 2025 ([EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:spr:perchp:978-981-95-8371-3_4); [Cambridge repository](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/handle/1810/338143?show=full)).
- **Paid IP services found:**
  - The TQI Intelligence Platform includes patent data, and TQI's due diligence has an "IP and patents" workstream ([TQI](https://thequantuminsider.com/quantum-computing-consulting/due-diligence/)).
  - H-Bar has assessed university IP portfolios at 15 institutions in Australia, the US and Europe ([H-Bar](https://www.h-bar.com.au/team)).
  - Law firms market filing and freedom-to-operate advice. Foley argues that "now is the window" to file foundational patents (advocacy) ([Foley](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2025/10/pivotal-push-from-lab-to-leverage-why-now-is-the-window-to-secure-foundational-quantum-patents/)).
- **Benchmarking:** Keysight acquired Quantum Benchmark Inc. (Kitchener, Ontario, backed by VanEdge and Quantonation, founded by Prof. Joseph Emerson) on 25 May 2021, for undisclosed terms. Quantum Benchmark sold error diagnostics, error suppression and performance-validation software, and it was Keysight's third quantum acquisition ([Business Wire, 25 May 2021](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20210525005748/en/Keysight-Technologies-Acquires-Quantum-Benchmark); [Waterloo](https://uwaterloo.ca/applied-mathematics/news/quantum-startup-founded-applied-mathematics-professors)).
- **Metriq** (Unitary Foundation) is a free community benchmarking platform with a composite "Metriq Score", released under CC BY 4.0 ([Metriq](https://metriq.info/)).
- Quantum has "no validation gate comparable to the FDA's, no accepted progress metric, and no norm of an independent scientific board" ([PostQuantum, Sept 2026](https://postquantum.com/quantum-commercialization/venture-due-diligence-decline/)).

### Inferences
- **Who pays:** for patents, mainly corporates and VCs through law firms and analytics suites, and universities for portfolio reviews. For benchmarks, mainly governments (DARPA-type programmes) and hardware vendors buying validation tools. An independent "certification" business has no regulatory mandate to force purchases.
- **The gap** is a public, continuously updated "claims vs. delivered" benchmark ledger linked to roadmaps and funding announcements. This is a credibility asset that could feed a media, intelligence or DD business, but it would struggle to be paid for on its own.
- **Strongest failure reasons:** free landscapes; patent analytics incumbents (PatSnap, Derwent, Questel-type suites, not researched here); the trust and physics-credential barrier for certification; and vendor hostility to unfavourable benchmarks.
- **Time to first revenue:** slow, 6-12+ months, unless bundled into DD or advisory work.

### Gaps
- Not covered: ISO/IEC JTC 3 status (the page returned HTTP 403), IEEE, ETSI and CEN-CENELEC quantum standards work, DARPA QBI, QED-C benchmark suites, and prices for quantum patent landscapes or freedom-to-operate opinions.

## 7. SUPPLY CHAIN / PROCUREMENT MARKETPLACES: do marketplaces exist, are there bottlenecks, and do brokers earn commissions?

### Takeaway
The bottlenecks are real and well documented: dilution refrigerators with 6-9 month lead times, helium-3 with no transparent pricing, lithium niobate wafers, electronic-grade diamond, Si-28 and rare earths. But procurement is high-value, low-volume, export-controlled and relationship-driven. A marketplace already exists inside TQI's ecosystem (QHub: 60+ vendors, 400+ products, with export-rule filters), and mapping efforts exist from MIT CTL, NIST MATTR, Sandia and NATO. I found no evidence that brokers or marketplaces earn meaningful commissions. For a zero-capital founder this is likely a false good idea, except perhaps as a niche used-equipment or helium-3 price-tracking information product.

### Cited Findings
- **Cryogenics:**
  - Bluefors has shipped 1,500+ dilution refrigerators and 15,000 cryocoolers. Its Syracuse (NY) site has capacity for about 20 systems a year, plus 50 assembly bays in Europe.
  - Lead times run 6-9 months at about one system per day of throughput. Bluefors delivered 18 systems to Japan's AIST in six weeks in 2024 as a surge.
  - A disruption "could halt U.S. superconducting development within months".
  - Pulse tubes come from Cryomech and SunPower (US), Sumitomo (Japan) and Europe.
  - Source: [War on the Rocks, Oct 2025](https://warontherocks.com/2025/10/the-supply-chain-chokepoints-in-quantum/).
  - Other estimates put lead times at 6-12 months. Oxford Instruments reports a 50% lead-time reduction, informally 5-8 months ([PostQuantum on cryogenic infrastructure](https://postquantum.com/building-quantum-computers/quantum-cryogenic-infrastructure-helium3/)).
- **Helium-3:**
  - There is no transparent pricing and there are no reliable long-term contracts. The only identified secure new source is a DOE agreement with Interlune (lunar extraction) ([War on the Rocks](https://warontherocks.com/2025/10/the-supply-chain-chokepoints-in-quantum/)).
  - Bluefors agreed in September 2025 to buy up to 10,000 litres a year for delivery from 2028 to 2037 ([Bluefors](https://bluefors.com/?p=11977); a "$300M" figure appears only on a forum profile and is unreliable).
  - Kiutra raised €13M to remove helium-3 dependency (via search snippet, [PostQuantum on helium-3 alternatives](https://postquantum.com/quantum-computing/helium-3-alternatives/)).
- **Other chokepoints:**
  - Lithium niobate wafers: Sumitomo Metal Mining and CASTECH dominate, and China controls about 60-70% of high-quality boules. Building US capacity would cost an estimated $100-300M.
  - Element Six is the only proven Western supplier of electronic-grade diamond.
  - Si-28 purification is specialised.
  - No dedicated quantum 300mm fab line exists.
  - NATO's 2025 study flags ion pumps, pulse tubes and photodetectors as single points of failure.
  - Source: [War on the Rocks](https://warontherocks.com/2025/10/the-supply-chain-chokepoints-in-quantum/).
- **Mapping and directories:**
  - **QHub** (hosted at hub.thequantuminsider.com) aggregates 60+ verified vendors and 400+ products across cryogenics, control electronics and photonics. It offers cross-vendor comparison and filtering by export rules. Its operator, business model and fees are not disclosed ([TQI, 11 Nov 2025](https://thequantuminsider.com/2025/11/11/everything-you-need-to-know-about-the-quantum-supply-chain-hub-qhub/)).
  - TQI's market map names suppliers: Oxford Instruments, Bluefors, ICE, FormFactor and Montana Instruments for cryogenics; Vescent, Toptica, Coherent and others for lasers ([TQI market map, Mar 2025](https://thequantuminsider.com/2025/03/19/the-quantum-supply-chain-mapping-the-market-and-key-players/)).
  - MIT CTL mapped 160 companies across four tiers ([MIT CTL](https://ctl.mit.edu/mapping-risks-quantum-computing-supply-chain)). NIST MATTR has been used to identify quantum suppliers ([NIST](https://www.nist.gov/blogs/manufacturing-innovation-blog/building-quantum-technology-supply-chain-mattr)). One survey counted 744 suppliers in 36 countries across 60 component types (search snippet, [PostQuantum supply-chain survey](https://postquantum.com/quantum-policy/quantum-supply-chain-survey/)). Sandia surveyed commodity supply in 2022 ([Sandia](https://sandia.gov/research/publications/details/survey-of-the-worldwide-supply-chain-of-commodities-needed-for-a-quantum-te-2022-09-01)).

### Inferences
- **Who pays:** hardware startups and labs buy directly from a few OEMs on 6-12 month quotes. OEMs have no need to pay commissions to a marketplace when demand exceeds supply. A seller's market undermines brokers.
- **A possible niche** is used and refurbished cryogenic and test equipment resale, or a He-3 and DR lead-time price index. Both are untested, and used-equipment brokerage needs capital, logistics and export licensing.
- **Strongest failure reasons:** tiny transaction count; long sales cycles; export controls (EU 2025/2003 now covers cryogenic cooling systems and wafer probers, so brokers face licensing duties); OEM-direct relationships; TQI's existing QHub; and no working capital for inventory.
- **Time to first revenue:** slow, 6-18 months, and uncertain.

### Gaps
- I found no evidence of commission rates, transaction volumes, or revenue for any quantum marketplace, including QHub. I also found no data on used dilution-refrigerator resale prices, or on isotope (Si-28, Yb-171) brokers.

## 8. INVESTOR DUE DILIGENCE / CLAIMS VERIFICATION: do investors, family offices, corporates or journalists pay for independent technical DD, who provides it, at what rates?

### Takeaway
Demand drivers are strong in 2025-2026:
- quantum equities trading at high multiples (Kerrisdale cited 40x 2026 consensus revenue for IonQ in March 2025);
- contested short reports (Kerrisdale 2025, Wolfpack 2026, Iceberg on QCi 2024);
- delivered revenue far below 2021 SPAC projections;
- the new COINS/OISP diligence duties.

But the market is structurally thin. Specialist quantum DD firms "are small, because nothing requires investors to hire them". Incumbents exist: TQI Advisory (weeks-long engagements, unpublished prices), H-Bar (more than $100M of investments diligenced since 2016), VC funds with in-house academic councils, and generalist expert networks (GLG, AlphaSights, Guidepoint) paying experts about $250-500/hr. Credibility, meaning a physics PhD and a track record, is the gating asset. A no-brand, AI-driven founder cannot sell signed technical opinions, but could sell structured "claims vs. evidence" research packs to buy-side analysts, journalists and family offices, ideally co-signed by a contracted physicist.

### Cited Findings
- **Short reports and disputes:**
  - Kerrisdale (March 2025) called IonQ a "cash-burning, highly promotional company... valued at absurd revenue multiples" (40x consensus 2026 revenue). It also challenged IonQ's past qubit claims ([Stocktwits](https://stocktwits.com/news-articles/markets/equity/ionq-stock-slides-on-kerrisdale-short-report/ch7dju4RbRv)).
  - Wolfpack (February 2026) alleged that about 86% of IonQ's 2022-2024 recognised revenue came from Pentagon "backdoor earmarks" (about $65.5M) and that insiders sold about $400M of stock. IonQ called the claims "false, misleading, and unsubstantiated" ([Fortune, 4 Feb 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/02/04/ionq-wolfpack-research-short-seller-says-quantum-computing-company-misled-investors-about-cancelled-government-backdoor-earmarks/); [DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/ionq-disputes-claims-from-short-seller-that-lost-government-contracts-have-left-546m-black-hole-in-companys-revenue/)).
- **Track record vs. promises:**
  - Three 2021 quantum SPACs projected about $1.2B of combined 2025 revenue and delivered about $138M.
  - IonQ's 2025 revenue was $130M against $237M projected in its 2021 deck.
  - Rigetti's Q2 2025 revenue was $1.8M and D-Wave's $3.1M.
  - An Iceberg Research short report (November 2024) alleged problems at QCi. These are unproven allegations, and class actions followed.
  - The only rigorous public quantum technical review the author found was Australia's government assessment of PsiQuantum (FOI release, March 2026).
  - "Specialist quantum diligence firms exist but are small, because nothing requires investors to hire them."
  - The author recommends LPs require independent feasibility reviews.
  - Source: [PostQuantum, 23 Sept 2026](https://postquantum.com/quantum-commercialization/venture-due-diligence-decline/). Conflict: the author's firm, Applied Quantum, sells technical DD.
- **Providers:**
  - **TQI Advisory** covers six workstreams (technology readiness, team, IP, competition, market sizing, traction) and runs "in weeks", with prices scoped individually. Its staff include a strategy director with 20+ PE commercial DD projects ([TQI DD page, Q4 2026](https://thequantuminsider.com/quantum-computing-consulting/due-diligence/)).
  - **H-Bar** (Profs. Simon Devitt, UTS, and Jared Cole, RMIT) has done technical DD on more than $100M of investments for more than two dozen startups since 2016. No prices are published ([H-Bar](https://www.h-bar.com.au/team)).
  - **Firgun Ventures** runs in-house DD with an academic advisory council ([EverythingStartups](https://www.everythingstartups.com/vc-funds/firgun-ventures)).
  - **1q3** (Philipp Kammerlander, Zurich) offered DD at a 2024 Swiss industry day ([b2match](https://www.b2match.com/e/qidis24/opportunities/UGFydGljaXBhdGlvbk9wcG9ydHVuaXR5OjEyMjA1MA==)).
  - **Freshfields** notes that M&A diligence increasingly covers post-quantum risk (HNDL, cryptographic inventories, PQC migration plans) ([Freshfields](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/technology-quotient/quantum-disentangled-2-quantum-and-ma-risks-and-opportunities-102mkds)).
- **Expert networks:**
  - AlphaSights pays experts "an agreed hourly rate, pro-rated" ([AlphaSights](https://www.alphasights.com/experts/)). GLG lets experts set their own hourly rate ([GLG](https://glginsights.com/network-member-recruitment/)).
  - Anecdotal expert rates are $250-500/hr ([Blind](https://www.teamblind.com/post/expert-hourly-rate-for-acquisition-dd-nwew1ewx); [Expert Opportunities](https://expertopportunities.com/expert-network-hourly-rates/)).
  - Clients are billed per call ([CleverX](https://cleverx.com/blog/expert-networks-explained-how-companies-connect-with-industry-specialist/)).

### Inferences
- **Who pays:** VC and PE funds before large rounds, corporate venture and M&A teams, family offices entering public quantum names, hedge funds (long and short), and governments. Journalists rarely pay.
- **Rates:** individual experts earn $250-500/hr via networks. Boutique engagements likely run five figures (inferred, not sourced).
- **Gap:** a repeatable, lower-cost "quantum claims dossier" product. It would cover roadmap-vs-delivery history, qubit and fidelity claims vs. peer-reviewed evidence, revenue quality, and COINS/OISP exposure. It could be sold per company (perhaps $1-5k) or by subscription to analysts. AI agents can assemble the evidence base quickly from filings, arXiv, press releases and patent data.
- **Defensibility:** depends entirely on accuracy and reputation. One public error kills the brand, and legal risk exists when writing about listed companies, including defamation and market-manipulation allegations if positions are held.
- **Time to first revenue:** 1-3 months for a first paid dossier if the founder can reach buy-side analysts, for instance through Substack or X. Without a recognised physicist co-signer, institutional buyers will default to TQI, H-Bar or expert networks.
- **Strongest failure reasons:** the trust barrier; investors not being required to buy DD; free short reports and media coverage; expert networks already supplying cheap access to physicists; and liability.

### Gaps
- No published prices exist for quantum DD engagements (TQI, H-Bar, Applied Quantum). I found no data on how often VCs commission external quantum DD, and no Guidepoint, Tegus or AlphaSense quantum-specific figures.

## 9. Across categories: small or bootstrapped players, shortest path to first paying customer, and "false good ideas"

### Takeaway
The only clearly documented small operators are a 6-person ad-funded media site (Quantum Zeitgeist), a two-academic DD consultancy (H-Bar), specialist recruiters embedded in larger groups (DeepRec in Trinnovo), and independent analysts whose sites can lapse (Fact Based Insight's domain now hosts gambling content). The category leader for information products, TQI/Resonance, is an 11-50 person company that has absorbed news, data, advisory, education (Q-munity), marketing services and a supplier marketplace (QHub). It is the main competitive threat to almost every idea here. On the evidence, the shortest paths to a first paid euro or dollar for an AI-leveraged, zero-capital founder are, in order:
1. tender and grant monitoring plus proposal support for quantum SMEs;
2. regulatory "readiness" briefings and checklists (EU 2025/2003, COINS/OISP) sold to VCs, clusters and SMEs;
3. company "claims dossiers" for investors.

The structurally weakest ("false good ideas") are a supplier marketplace, standalone patent analytics or certification, a generic quantum news site, and generic quantum courses.

### Cited Findings
- **Small players:**
  - Quantum Zeitgeist: Hadamard LLC, six named staff, ads and partnerships, 18,000+ articles ([About](https://quantumzeitgeist.com/about/)).
  - H-Bar: two named professors, DD on more than $100M of deals since 2016 ([H-Bar](https://www.h-bar.com.au/team)).
  - Fact Based Insight's domain now serves unrelated gambling content (fetched 22 November 2026) ([factbasedinsight.com](https://www.factbasedinsight.com/)).
  - Grantable, a lightly funded AI grant tool (about $100K raised), sells at $50-150/month ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/grantable/); [pricing](https://grantable.co/pricing)).
- **Consolidator:** TQI/Resonance has 11-50 staff, 200+ clients, datasets at $2,999-5,999, and spans platform, advisory, marketing services, education (Q-munity, 2024) and QHub ([About](https://thequantuminsider.com/about/); [datasets](https://thequantuminsider.com/datasets/guides/dataset-or-platform-which-do-i-need/); [Q-munity](https://thequantuminsider.com/2024/05/02/accelerating-quantum-education-resonance-subsidiary-the-quantum-insider-invests-in-q-munity-leader-in-workforce-development/); [QHub](https://thequantuminsider.com/2025/11/11/everything-you-need-to-know-about-the-quantum-supply-chain-hub-qhub/)).
- **Funding concentration:** Market revenue was $1.4B in 2025 against $4.9B of VC ([PostQuantum citing QED-C](https://postquantum.com/quantum-commercialization/venture-due-diligence-decline/)). Most quantum firms have 11-200 staff ([LinkedIn, Oct 2025](https://economicgraph.linkedin.com/content/dam/me/economicgraph/en-us/PDF/Quantum-Talent-Trends-in-an-Emerging-Workforce.pdf)). The UK sector is about 1,700 people across 160+ companies ([UK Quantum Skills Taskforce](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/uk-quantum-skills-taskforce-report/uk-quantum-skills-taskforce-report)).
