# Zero-capital, AI-agent-operated businesses in deep-tech / quantum markets: what works, what fails (state as of Nov 2026)

Method note: research ran on 22 Nov 2026. The shared web-search budget ran out partway through, so several sub-questions (FTC "quantum" health-product actions, regulatory-change-monitoring SaaS pricing, tender-alert pricing, paid-community and cohort-course benchmarks, MIT/NANDA-type GenAI pilot failure data, hiQ v LinkedIn final outcome, CV-Online Latvia text, entity costs outside the UK) could not be checked and are listed under Gaps. Every bullet with a figure carries its source; figures from vendor blogs or aggregators are flagged as low-confidence.

## 1. Base rates and case studies: solo or tiny bootstrapped businesses in quantum and comparable frontier niches

### Takeaway
Bootstrapped quantum businesses that have lasted are almost all media, data or training plays that started from free content 5 to 10 years ago (QCR in 2015, Quantum Zeitgeist in 2018, Ezratty's book in 2018, Quantum Daily/TQI around 2019, QURECA in 2019). None of them publishes revenue. The best-documented solo asset, QCR, charged only $100 a year per person before it was folded into a multi-founder intelligence firm (GQI). Software plays (qBraid, BlueQubit) needed grants and/or venture money and PhD-level founders. The documented "AI-era solo" successes (Base44) are outside quantum, rarely truly solo, and heavily affected by survivorship bias.

### Inferences
- The surviving quantum media and data franchises took about 3 to 7 years to move from free content to paid data or advisory (TQI: ~2019 content to 2022 holding company and data business; QCR: 2015 to the 2022 GQI merger). Realistic time to *meaningful* revenue for a newcomer in quantum media/data is years, not months. Time to *first* revenue (ads, a sponsored post, a job post) can be short, but the amounts are small.
- QCR's prices ($100/yr individual, $1,000/yr for 100 seats) after seven years as the reference database suggest that subscription revenue from quantum *individuals* is capped low. The value was captured by bundling with advisory (GQI) and selling to corporates and governments (TQI). The paying customer is an institution, not a reader.
- Credentials by model, on the evidence gathered: training/advisory (QURECA) and software (qBraid, BlueQubit, Quantum Flytrap) were led by physics PhDs or graduates of elite quantum programmes. Media, data and job boards (TQI, Quantum Zeitgeist, Quantum Jobs List) show no evidence that a PhD was needed. Ezratty shows that sustained, exhaustive free content can build analyst authority.
- The niche is already crowded with incumbents (GQI/QCR, TQI, Quantum Zeitgeist, IQT, QURECA, Ezratty, TQI's job board and Quantum Jobs List). A new generic "quantum news/data/jobs" entrant starts with no distribution advantage.
- Survivorship bias is severe. Every success here is visible because it survived, and no base rate for failed quantum newsletters or blogs exists.

### Gaps
- No verified revenue for QCR (before or after the merger), TQI/Resonance, QURECA, Quantum Zeitgeist, IQT or Ezratty. UK Companies House filings for QURECA were not checked.
- Formal credentials of Finke, Challans/Kubes and Ezratty were not verified in this session.
- No data found on solo quantum YouTubers or Substack newsletters (subscriber counts, paid conversion), or on small PQC consultancies' revenue.
- No base rate exists for how many quantum media or data ventures were started and abandoned.

## 2. Failures and "false good ideas" (including regulatory warnings)

### Takeaway
Documented quantum failures and stalls cluster in four places:
1. Over-promised commercial readiness (Zapata's SPAC collapse, Arqit's revenue restatement problems, QCi's alleged overstatements).
2. Thin-margin access, education or visualisation tools with very few payers (Quantum Flytrap).
3. Commoditised "quantum" utilities that universities or big vendors supply for free or near-free (QRNG APIs, readiness self-assessments).
4. Jurisdiction and policy risk (Nordic QCG).

The word "quantum" is also heavily polluted by retail-investment scams ("Quantum AI"), which regulators on several continents have flagged repeatedly from 2021 to 2026. "Quantum + AI + trading" branding is a reputational liability.

### Cited Findings
- **Zapata AI** (Harvard spinout founded 2017, quantum software that pivoted to "AI"): listed via SPAC with Andretti Acquisition Corp in 2023 and ceased operations on 9 Oct 2024 because it could not meet financial obligations. — [TQI, 12 Oct 2024](https://thequantuminsider.com/2024/10/12/zapata-computing-holdings-inc-ceases-operations/). The SPAC was meant to bring $100M of equity but brought "effectively zero" plus "$20-plus millions of liability". — [The Next Platform, 12 Mar 2026](https://www.nextplatform.com/compute/2026/03/12/four-months-into-its-comeback-zapata-stakes-its-claim-in-quantum-software/5209179). It re-emerged as Zapata Quantum after bankruptcy in Sept 2025. — [TQI, 3 Sep 2025](https://thequantuminsider.com/2025/09/03/viva-zapata-zapata-quantum-reemerges-after-bankruptcy-filing-with-restructuring-and-new-financing/). It raised $15M in Apr 2026. — [TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/zapata-quantum-15m-financing)
- **Nordic Quantum Computing Group** (Norway, hardware): ceased operations on 6 Dec 2024. It cited no national quantum strategy, weak infrastructure, no startup visa, a retroactive 37.84% exit tax on returning employees, the wealth tax and a new exit tax. — [TQI, 10 Dec 2024](https://thequantuminsider.com/2024/12/10/nordic-quantum-computing-group-shuts-down-citing-government-policies-taxes-lack-of-support/)
- **Quantum Flytrap** (education, visualisation and UI tooling): closed in Oct 2022 after about three years. Its products were a browser quantum simulation engine, the Virtual Lab (used at Oxford and Stanford, 700+ user-created experiments) and Pulser Studio, built for Pasqal, which is the only named commercial customer. The founders were a designer and a quantum physicist/AI specialist. No reasons or financials were given. — [Quantum Flytrap closing post](https://quantumflytrap.com/blog/closing-quantum-flytrap)
- **Arqit** (quantum-safe encryption): in 2022 press reports said it overstated revenue and readiness, and its CRO resigned. The Times reported that most of the "committed revenue" pitched to investors was research grants. — [Capacity Media](https://capacitymedia.com/article/29zvx3vhdiqk09vom3nk0/news/arqit-fights-back-after-critics-send-share-price-down-43-in-a-month); [Quantum Zeitgeist: Arqit commercial history](https://quantumzeitgeist.com/arqit-arqq/). Revenue fell 64% in one year. A Simply Wall St summary (aggregator, unverified) reports a H1 2026 net loss of about US$33.1M on revenue of about US$1.2M, with the share count roughly quadrupled since the SPAC. — [Simply Wall St](https://simplywall.st/stocks/us/software/nasdaq-arqq/arqit-quantum)
- **Quantum Computing Inc. (QUBT)**: a securities class action (D.N.J. 2:25-cv-01457, filed 25 Feb 2025) alleges it overstated its quantum capabilities and partner relationships. It followed an Iceberg Research report of 27 Nov 2024 alleging its TFLN foundry claims were a "sham". These are allegations, not findings. — [Complaint PDF](https://rosenlegal.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Quantum-Computing-Complaint-Web-4930-3255-4784-v.1.pdf); [Kessler Topaz](https://www.ktmc.com/new-cases/quantum-computing-inc)
- **QC Ware** (~$60M raised, per an aggregator): launched Promethium, a GPU-accelerated quantum-chemistry SaaS, on AWS Marketplace in Apr 2023. A secondary dossier says the part that "pays the bills" runs on NVIDIA GPUs, not quantum hardware. — [HPCwire, 17 Apr 2023](https://www.hpcwire.com/2023/04/17/qc-ware-launches-promethium-a-quantum-chemistry-saas-targets-hybrid-world-near-term/); [yespress dossier](https://yespress.io/qc-ware-corp)
- **Quantum random-number APIs are commoditised**: ANU's QRNG launched on AWS Marketplace in 2022 at US$0.005 per request with a free tier of 100 requests/month. Competing listings such as SeQRNG charge $0.01 per request. — [ITPro](https://www.itpro.com/cloud/cloud-computing/367492/aws-launches-quantum-random-number-generator); [AWS Marketplace listing](https://aws.amazon.com/marketplace/pp/B09SFWPDRJ)
- **Generic "quantum readiness" assessments are commoditised**: free self-serve tools exist, such as Encryption Consulting's 20-question PQC maturity test and postquantum.com's free self-assessment. Large firms sell branded versions (KPMG Q-PREP, Palo Alto Networks, Thales "PQC migration starter pack"), and TQI sells scoped readiness assessments too. — [postquantum.com self-assessment](https://postquantum.com/post-quantum/free-pqc-readiness-self-assessment/); [KPMG Q-PREP](https://kpmg.com/us/en/capabilities-services/advisory-services/cyber-security-services/cyber-strategy-governance/kpmg-q-prep-postquantum-cryptography-readiness.html); [Palo Alto](https://www.paloaltonetworks.com/network-security/quantum-readiness-assessment); [Thales](https://www.thalesgroup.com/en/solutions-catalogue/cybersecurity/cybersecurity-services/post-quantum-cryptography-migration); [TQI readiness assessment](https://thequantuminsider.com/quantum-computing-consulting/readiness-assessment/)

### Inferences
- **False good idea: "quantum + AI + trading/finance signals" products.** The brand space is saturated with regulator-flagged scams, and any retail-facing financial product with that branding inherits suspicion. It may also trigger financial-promotion regulation (FCA authorisation).
- **False good idea: QRNG resale or API wrappers.** Supply is free or cheap from universities and big clouds at fractions of a cent per call, leaving no margin for a solo reseller.
- **False good idea: generic "quantum readiness" PDFs or assessments.** These are free from multiple vendors and bundled by Big Four firms and incumbents like TQI. Only a niche-specific, evidence-producing deliverable (for example an actual cryptographic inventory for one regulated vertical) escapes commoditisation, and that requires security expertise and liability cover (see Q8).
- **False good idea: consumer quantum education apps or games.** The best-documented attempt (Quantum Flytrap, with a strong team and Oxford/Stanford adoption) closed after about three years with one named commercial client.
- **"Grants as revenue" is a known red flag** (Arqit's "committed revenue"). Grants are fine as first cash but should not be presented as commercial traction.
- **Pivoting a quantum company's branding to "AI"** (Zapata) did not save it. Branding plays do not substitute for paying customers.

### Gaps
- No specific documented failures of "quantum app stores/marketplaces" or quantum cloud resellers were found. Strangeworks, Agnostiq/Covalent and similar pivots were not checked.
- Quantum-themed crypto tokens (for example "quantum-resistant" coins) were not assessed.
- FTC actions on "quantum" health/energy product claims could not be checked (search budget exhausted).
- Hong Kong SFC's 2024 "Quantum AI" deepfake warning was seen only through secondary coverage.

## 3. What AI agents reliably do for a one-person company in 2026, and where they fail

### Takeaway
Rigorous evidence shows real but bounded capability. On realistic multi-step office tasks the best agents complete roughly a quarter to a third autonomously. In a controlled trial, experienced developers were *slower* with AI while believing they were faster. Gartner expects over 40% of agentic projects to be cancelled by 2027. Hallucinated citations in a paid government report cost Deloitte part of an AU$440K contract. For a solo founder selling *technical* intelligence or compliance outputs, unsupervised agent output is a liability. Agents add the most value as throughput multipliers under expert review. Cloning cost has collapsed: a major framework was reportedly rebuilt in about five days for about $1,100 of tokens, so AI-built software alone is not defensible.

### Cited Findings
- **METR randomised trial** (16 experienced open-source developers, Feb-Jun 2025 tools, mainly Cursor with Claude 3.5/3.7 Sonnet): tasks took about 19% longer with AI allowed. The developers had forecast a 24% speed-up and afterwards believed they had been 20% faster. AI-assisted time shifted to reviewing, prompting and waiting. METR calls it a "point-in-time" measurement. — [METR, 10 Jul 2025](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/)
- **TheAgentCompany benchmark** (CMU; 175 realistic tasks in a simulated software company involving browsing, coding and colleague interaction): the best agent completes 30% of tasks autonomously per the paper. CMU's news story reports 24%, rising to 34.4% with partial credit. Long-horizon tasks remain "beyond reach". — [arXiv 2412.14161](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14161); [CMU News, 2025](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/news/2025/agent-company)
- **Gartner, 25 Jun 2025**: over 40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by end-2027 because of cost, unclear value or inadequate risk controls. "Agent washing" is widespread, and only about 130 of thousands of "agentic" vendors are real. Gartner still predicts that 15% of day-to-day work decisions will be autonomous by 2028. — [Gartner press release](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-predicts-over-40-percent-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-by-end-of-2027)
- **Deloitte Australia, Oct 2025**: an AU$440K review for the Department of Employment and Workplace Relations contained more than a dozen fabricated references, a fictional extract from *Amato v Commonwealth* and a made-up quote from a Federal Court judge. The methodology note disclosed an Azure OpenAI GPT-4o toolchain. A University of Sydney academic found the errors after publication, and Deloitte repaid the final instalment. — [The Register, 6 Oct 2025](https://www.theregister.com/2025/10/06/deloitte_ai_report_australia/); [Fortune, 7 Oct 2025](https://fortune.com/2025/10/07/deloitte-ai-australia-government-report-hallucinations-technology-290000-refund/)
- **Cloning speed**: a Cloudflare engineer reportedly rebuilt Next.js with an AI model over more than 800 sessions, about five days and about $1,100 in tokens, reaching 94% API coverage (secondary blog account). — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/rentierdigital/your-saas-is-one-week-and-1100-away-from-being-cloned-cloudflare-just-proved-it-4ba6). Base44's founder warns that vibe-coding startups face "big copycat risk" and that features can be copied "in weeks or months". — [AOL/Business Insider](https://www.aol.com/articles/vibe-coding-startups-face-big-060944200.html)
- **Security quality of AI code**: one benchmark cited in a secondary piece found AI-generated solutions 61% functionally correct but only 10.5% secure. The primary paper was not verified. — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/rentierdigital/your-saas-is-one-week-and-1100-away-from-being-cloned-cloudflare-just-proved-it-4ba6)
- **"Solo" success stories often have teams**: Base44 had eight employees at exit. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/18/six-month-old-solo-owned-vibe-coder-base44-sells-to-wix-for-80m-cash). Solo-founded companies raise only about 20% of VC rounds. — [Carta](https://carta.com/data/solo-founders-report)
- **Automated content at scale already exists in quantum**: Quantum Zeitgeist reports 18,000+ articles since 2018 from a small LLC and gives all content away free. — [Quantum Zeitgeist About](https://quantumzeitgeist.com/about-us/)

### Inferences
- **Reliable enough with human review**: code scaffolding and internal tools; continuous monitoring and structured extraction of public sources (funding announcements, tenders, job posts, standards updates) feeding a database; first drafts of digests and reports; outbound personalisation at volume; tier-1 support.
- **Not reliable unsupervised**: anything with citations or legal or technical claims a customer relies on (compliance mapping, PQC exposure statements, "quantum advantage" assessments); long-horizon multi-system tasks; relationship selling to institutions; anything carrying professional liability.
- Because agent-written quantum news is already abundant and free (Quantum Zeitgeist's volume, TQI's free tier), "AI-generated quantum news" is not a product. A curated, verified and *accountable* dataset or judgement is what can be sold.
- The METR result implies founders systematically overestimate their AI speed-up. Plans should budget review time explicitly.

### Gaps
- No verified, quantum- or deep-tech-specific examples were found of one-person companies at $100K-$1M ARR run mainly by agents. Public lists of such cases (Indie Hackers, Starter Story) were not reachable after the search budget ran out.
- No hard data was gathered on deliverability or reply rates of AI-automated outbound in 2026, or on platform bans (LinkedIn automation).
- MIT/NANDA-style "GenAI pilot failure rate" data was not verified.

## 4. Defensibility without capital in the AI era

### Takeaway
The moats with the most evidence in this niche are:
- **Accumulated proprietary data plus the distribution/audience that feeds it** (TQI: media, then a database of 2,100+ companies sold at $2,999+ per dataset; QCR: a database since 2015 that became the asset merged into GQI).
- **Brand and trust as a neutral reference** (Ezratty's free annual book; QCR).
- **Workflow embedding** (qBraid moving from user sandbox to "qBraid-OS" inside four hardware companies).

Code and features are no longer a moat. Data moats are contested, and incumbents' free tiers erode them. Regulatory and procurement listings (for example UK G-Cloud) act as a channel more than a moat.

### Cited Findings
- **NFX (Pete Flint)**: "typical" defensibility strategies are too slow in the AI era. Distribution and brand, real-time data and workflow embedding (for example EvenUp's demand letters for personal-injury lawyers) are the AI-era levers. Companies that fail to convert early speed into "motte" defensibility tend to die (undated in results). — [NFX: AI defensibility](https://nfx.com/post/ai-defensibility)
- **Data-moat scepticism**: a16z argues data moats are often an "empty promise", because data value saturates and is replicable (2019). — [a16z](https://a16z.com/the-empty-promise-of-data-moats/). Insight Partners counters that access to "specific, often messy and unstandardised data" remains one of the strongest moats for vertical AI. — [Insight Partners](https://insightpartners.com/ideas/building-a-moat-in-the-age-of-ai)
- **SignalFire (May 2026)**: at pre-seed to Series A, moats are "mostly theoretical". Defensibility is earned by surviving several waves of model progress ("permanence"). — [SignalFire](https://www.signalfire.com/blog/why-ai-startups-should-optimize-for-permanence-not-moats)
- **Code is not a moat**: features are cloned within days or weeks (see Q3). Practitioners argue that maintenance, compliance, integrations, support, switching costs, community and distribution are harder to copy. — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/vibe-coding-is-quietly-killing-simple-saas-products-the-thing-thats-actually-defensible-isn-t-a-feature-it-s-the-boring-layer-3a785dadf1); [DEV Community](https://dev.to/rentierdigital/your-saas-is-one-week-and-1100-away-from-being-cloned-cloudflare-just-proved-it-4ba6)
- **Quantum data moat in practice**: TQI's Intelligence Platform (2,100+ companies, 2,000+ investors, 1,300+ funding rounds, patents, national programmes, daily updates, API) is sold per seat annually, with standalone datasets at $2,999 to $5,999. — [TQI](https://thequantuminsider.com/datasets/guides/dataset-or-platform-which-do-i-need/). TQI built it on an earlier media audience and moved "from writing to data". — [QCR podcast](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/podcast-with-alex-challans-ceo-of-resonance/)
- **Counter-evidence (incumbents give data away)**: TQI has announced free public access to its data platform (headline only; date not verified). — [TQI](https://thequantuminsider.com/?p=9204). QCR gives a free GQI Portal preview to all accounts. — [QCR About](https://www.quantumcomputingreport.com/about/)
- **Brand and neutrality**: Ezratty's 1,500-page free, ungated annual book, updated since 2018, underpins his advisory and training work. — [Ezratty](https://www.oezratty.net/wordpress/2025/understanding-quantum-technologies-2025/print/)
- **Workflow embedding**: qBraid moved from an education/sandbox product (20,000+ users) to "qBraid-OS", used by four leading quantum companies that "want to focus on hardware". — [MIT News, 4 Nov 2025](https://news.mit.edu/2025/startup-provides-nontechnical-gateway-coding-quantum-computers-1104)
- **Procurement listing as a channel**: Axis12 lists PQC assessments with public prices on UK G-Cloud 15, and its day rates include PI insurance. — [Axis12 G-Cloud 15 pricing document, Jan 2026](https://assets.applytosupply.digitalmarketplace.service.gov.uk/g-cloud-15/documents/92214/380441517909314-pricing-document-2026-01-29-1702.pdf)
- **Legal protection of a curated dataset (EU)**: under *Ryanair v PR Aviation* (C-30/14, Jan 2015), if a database has neither copyright nor sui generis protection, the owner can still restrict scraping by contract (website terms). Protection of the founder's own dataset therefore depends on showing "substantial investment" (sui generis right) or on contract. — [Pinsent Masons Out-Law](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/news/website-operators-can-prohibit-screen-scraping-of-unprotected-data-via-terms-and-conditions-says-eu-court-in-ryanair-case); [Kluwer Copyright Blog](https://legalblogs.wolterskluwer.com/copyright-blog/ryanair-ltd-v-pr-aviation-bv-contracts-rights-and-users-in-a-low-cost-database-law/)

### Inferences
- For a zero-capital newcomer, the only realistic moat stack is: (1) a narrow, verifiable dataset that compounds over time and that incumbents under-serve (for example a vertical or regional slice, or a data type they don't track), plus (2) owned distribution (a newsletter list or LinkedIn audience in that slice), plus (3) a reputation for accuracy. Each element alone is weak. Incumbents (TQI, GQI) can replicate a generic quantum dataset and already give parts of theirs away.
- Language and regional localisation (for example French-language or EU-programme-specific intelligence) is a *plausible* under-served slice. Gaps between incumbents (both English-language and US/Canada-centric: TQI's customers are mostly American) suggest room, but no direct evidence was found.
- Exclusive partnerships and associations (national quantum programmes, QED-C/QuIC-type consortia) are probably the strongest moat available to a tiny player, but they need trust built over time. No case evidence was gathered.

### Gaps
- No data on churn or retention of niche intelligence subscriptions in quantum.
- No evidence gathered on certification-based moats (for example NCSC Assured Consultancy, ANSSI PASSI qualification) or their cost and time.
- The date and terms of TQI's "free public access" announcement were not verified.

## 5. Selling into the quantum market: buyer pool, cycles, procurement

### Takeaway
The pure-play buyer pool is small and concentrated. QED-C counts 556 pure-play quantum companies (7,418 "quantum-engaged" organisations) and about 16,500 pure-play employees worldwide at end-2025. The total quantum market was about $1.9B in 2025. Capital is abundant but concentrated: $4.9B of VC (QED-C) or $12.6B of start-up investment including capital-market deals (McKinsey), 90% to quantum computing. The fastest, credit-card-sized purchases available to a tiny vendor are job posts, ads/sponsorships, datasets and memberships. Institutional buyers (corporates, governments, large CISOs) have the budgets but buy through scoped advisory, frameworks or long cycles. PQC compliance deadlines (EU: start by end-2026, high-risk by end-2030; UK NCSC: 2028/2031/2035; NIST IR 8547 final Oct 2026) create the broadest *affected-organisation* demand, but it mainly targets large organisations and critical infrastructure, and SMEs are expected to get PQC through their vendors.

### Cited Findings
- **Counts (QED-C 2026 report, via TQI, 14 Apr 2026)**: 7,418 quantum-engaged organisations and 556 pure-play companies at end-2025. The EU has 173 pure-plays and the US 164 (per search summary). The 2025 market was $1.9B (computing $1.4B, sensing $470M), forecast above $4B by 2028. VC in 2025 was $4.9B, with US companies at over $2.7B. Public funding commitments total about $56.7B. The pure-play workforce is about 16,500 (+14%), and job openings rose 11% with a shift towards business roles. More than half of companies expect at least 11% revenue growth in 2026. — [TQI on QED-C 2026](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/04/14/global-quantum-computing-market-to-double-by-2028-reaching-3-billion-in-revenue-qed-c-state-of-the-global-quantum-industry-2026-report-finds/)
- **McKinsey Quantum Technology Monitor 2026**: quantum start-up investment reached $12.6B in 2025, 6.3 times 2024, with 90% going to quantum computing. The public-source share fell from about one-third in 2024 to 3% in 2025. — [McKinsey 2026 Monitor](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/mckinsey-quantum-technology-monitor-2026-a-commercial-tipping-point)
- **EPO mapping**: 935 active quantum-technology-origin companies across 72 countries and jurisdictions (different methodology). — [EPO executive summary](https://link.epo.org/web/publications/studies/en-mapping-the-global-quantum-ecosystem-executive-summary.pdf). TQI tracks 2,100+ companies and 2,000+ investors. — [TQI](https://thequantuminsider.com/datasets/guides/dataset-or-platform-which-do-i-need/)
- **Who pays incumbents**: TQI's customers are large corporates and national governments, mostly American. — [QCR podcast](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/podcast-with-alex-challans-ceo-of-resonance/). TQI's readiness assessments target enterprises with long-lived confidential data, financial institutions and public bodies. — [TQI readiness](https://thequantuminsider.com/quantum-computing-consulting/readiness-assessment/)
- **What quantum startups outsource (evidence)**: software infrastructure and OS layers (qBraid-OS for four hardware companies) — [MIT News](https://news.mit.edu/2025/startup-provides-nontechnical-gateway-coding-quantum-computers-1104); UI and front-end tooling (Quantum Flytrap's Pulser Studio for Pasqal) — [Quantum Flytrap](https://quantumflytrap.com/blog/closing-quantum-flytrap); recruiting via paid job posts — [Quantum Jobs List](https://www.quantumjobslist.com/pricing)
- **PQC regulatory timeline (demand driver)**: the EU Coordinated Implementation Roadmap (adopted June 2025) says all Member States should start transitioning by end-2026 and high-risk use cases should migrate "no later than by the end of 2030". It is aimed at Member States' public sectors and "private entities serving public utilities", with no explicit company obligations. — [European Commission PQC page](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/post-quantum-cryptography). Secondary sources cite 2035 for remaining systems. — [Utimaco](https://utimaco.com/news/blog-posts/eus-roadmap-post-quantum-cryptography). CEPS criticises the roadmap for leaving enforcement to Member States. — [CEPS Task Force comments](https://cdn.ceps.eu/2025/10/Task-FORCE-Comments-on-the-NIS-CG-Roadmap-on-PQC-Transition_FINAL.pdf)
- **UK NCSC (20 Mar 2025)**: discovery and an initial plan by 2028, highest-priority migration by 2031, full migration by 2035. The guidance is aimed at large organisations, critical infrastructure and bespoke IT. SMEs on commodity IT should receive PQC "through their vendors". — [NCSC PQC timelines](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/guidance/pqc-migration-timelines)
- **US**: NIST IR 8547 (transition to PQC standards) was published as final in Oct 2026, superseding the Nov 2024 draft. It references Executive Order 14412 and OMB M-26-15, which direct federal agencies to prioritise migrating sensitive systems. — [NIST IR 8547 final](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/ir/8547/final)
- **Sales-cycle benchmarks (vendor blogs, low confidence)**: SMB deals under ~$15K ACV close in 14 to 30 days (median ~22). Mid-market runs 60 to 120 days (one RAIN Group-attributed median: 92 days in 2026, versus 68 in 2019). Enterprise runs 6 to 9 months, and deals above $250K run 9 to 18 months. — [Optif.ai](https://optif.ai/learn/questions/sales-cycle-length-benchmark/); [Prospeo](https://prospeo.io/s/sales-cycle-length); [Stackmatix](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/b2b-sales-cycle-benchmarks)
- **Public-sector purchase via framework**: UK G-Cloud 15 lists PQC services with public prices (Axis12), which lets public bodies buy without a full tender. — [Axis12 pricing](https://assets.applytosupply.digitalmarketplace.service.gov.uk/g-cloud-15/documents/92214/380441517909314-pricing-document-2026-01-29-1702.pdf)
- **Marketplace procurement**: AWS Marketplace charges no listing fee. The fee is 3% on public SaaS offers; private offers pay 3% (TCV under $1M), 2% ($1M to $10M) or 1.5% (above $10M); private-offer renewals pay 1.5%; channel-partner offers add 0.5%. — [AWS Marketplace listing fees docs](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/marketplace/latest/userguide/listing-fees.html); [AWS announcement, Jan 2024](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2024/01/aws-marketplace-simplified-reduced-listing-fees)

### Inferences
- An average of about 30 employees per pure-play (≈16,500 / 556) means most quantum companies are small R&D teams with limited budget for third-party services. Big spenders are a few dozen well-funded firms and governments. "Sell to quantum startups" therefore means a small number of logos, and churn risk follows the funding cycle.
- Quick closes at or under ~$5K by card are realistic for job posts (€200-€1,700), sponsorships ($100-$750 per placement for a few-thousand list), memberships ($40-$1,000/yr) and single datasets or reports (≤$3K). Anything sold to CISOs, governments or corporates as an assessment will follow mid-market or enterprise cycles (3 to 9+ months) and needs PI insurance and references.
- The PQC timelines make "organisations affected by quantum" (banks, critical infrastructure, public bodies, their suppliers) a far larger pool than quantum companies. These buyers mostly expect vendors, Big Four firms and accredited consultancies to deliver, and the NCSC explicitly routes SMEs to their vendors. A credential-light solo founder is more plausible as a supplier *to the vendors and consultancies* (data, monitoring, mapping tools) than as a direct advisor to CISOs.

### Gaps
- No data was found on university labs' or tech-transfer offices' purchasing (budgets, card limits), or on how corporate innovation teams or law firms buy quantum subject-matter input.
- No evidence was gathered on which buyer types accept card or self-serve purchases under $5K (inferred only from public price points).
- The EU/US pure-play split (173/164) comes from the search summary, not the fetched article text.

## 6. Pricing benchmarks for comparable niche B2B information and compliance products

### Takeaway
Observed price points in and around quantum (2025-2026):
- Memberships: $40 to $1,000/yr (QCR)
- Single datasets: $2,999 to $5,999 (TQI)
- Job posts: €200 to €230 per 40-day listing, or about €3 per click

The gradient is steep: reader/individual pricing is tiny and institutional pricing is 10 to 1,000 times higher.

## 7. First-revenue tactics with no budget: what is documented to work in technical niches

### Takeaway
Across 27 well-known B2B companies, the first 10 customers came mostly from the founders' personal networks (cited for 9), followed by investor networks, communities, targeted cold outbound, content, press and launches. Founders with no network in the target industry have to build trust from scratch, usually through content or community over months to years. In quantum, the documented path is free content leading to audience, then data, then institutional clients (TQI, QCR, Ezratty). Grants (NSF, DOE) and AWS Marketplace listings appear in the quantum case studies as early-cash or channel tactics.

### Cited Findings
- **Lenny's Newsletter (5 Sep 2023)**, tallied across 27 companies (some appear in more than one channel):

  | Channel | Companies cited |
  |---|---|
  | Personal network | 9 |
  | Investor networks | 4 |
  | Communities and networking | 4 |
  | Cold outbound | 3 |
  | Content and online following | 3 |
  | Press | 3 |
  | "Just launch" | 3 |

  — [Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-to-win-your-first-10-b2b-customers)

- **Grants as first cash**: qBraid had ~$0.3M from NSF. — [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/qbraid/financials). BlueQubit had a $1.5M DOE grant (third-party). — [Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/companies/bluequbit/). ### Inferences
- A founder with no quantum network should expect the "content, then audience, then trust" path to take 6 to 24 months before institutional buyers respond. Quantum incumbents took years, and they started in 2015 to 2019 when the field was less crowded.
- The quickest documented-style first revenue in this niche is small, transactional and self-serve: a paid job post, a sponsored slot, a dataset sale, or an AWS Marketplace or G-Cloud listing. The Vanta pattern (outreach to a precisely filtered list of people with a stated pain) adapts naturally to PQC deadline-driven buyers or to quantum startups that have just raised money (filtered from funding announcements).
- Grants and small public studies are plausible first cash, but they are slow (months) and should be treated as funding, not as validation of commercial demand.

### Gaps
- No measured timelines (days or months to first dollar) were found for these tactics in quantum or comparable technical niches.
- Small public tenders for quantum studies (EU, national programmes) and their typical size and eligibility were not searched (budget exhausted).
- Partnering with associations or consultancies as a channel: no documented quantum example was found.

## 8. Legal and operational constraints for a one-person international operation

### Takeaway
None of the constraints are prohibitive, but several are material for a solo operator:
- **Liability for advice**: AI-hallucinated deliverables are a documented failure mode. PI insurance costs roughly US$500 to $1,500 a year for solo consultants in the US.
- **Scraping**: US law has become more scraper-friendly for logged-off public data (*Meta v Bright Data*, 2024). In the EU, database rights and website terms (*Ryanair*) still bite.
- **Outbound email**: B2B email without consent is tolerated in France and the UK under conditions (relevance to the recipient's job, opt-out). GDPR still applies to named contacts, and the CNIL fines data brokers (CALOGA, €80K, 2025).
- **Export controls**: new US (Sept 2024) and EU (in force 15 Nov 2025) controls cover quantum computers, components and related "technology". This matters if the business handles non-public technical data or serves restricted countries.
- **Entity cost**: low (a UK Ltd costs £100 to incorporate online since 1 Feb 2026).

### Cited Findings
- **Liability**: Deloitte's AU$440K report contained fabricated citations and a fake judicial quote produced with a GPT-4o toolchain, leading to a partial refund and public criticism (Oct 2025). — [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/10/06/deloitte_ai_report_australia/)
- **PI insurance cost**:
  - US consultants average about $62/month, with policies from about $400 to $3,750+ a year. — [Insureon](https://www.insureon.com/consulting-business-insurance/cost)

### Inferences
- **Export controls**: a business built on *public* market intelligence (funding, jobs, policy, company profiles) is low-exposure. A business that handles or transmits non-public technical specifications, design data or "development/production technology" of quantum hardware, or provides technical assistance to companies in restricted destinations, could trigger US or EU licensing. This includes some consulting for hardware startups or technical due diligence. Legal review is needed before offering technical services across borders.
- **Liability**: any compliance or security deliverable (PQC exposure, regulatory mapping) needs human expert sign-off, explicit disclaimers, PI insurance, and ideally positioning as "information" rather than "advice". Information and data products carry less liability than assessments.
- **Outbound**: agent-driven B2B email is legally feasible in the UK and France if it is relevant to the recipient's role, offers an opt-out and keeps a suppression list. Buying or scraping contact lists from brokers is the higher-risk step, as CALOGA and NESTOR show.
- **Entity**: a minimal company is cheap (UK £100, Wyoming LLC). Running costs (accounting, confirmation statements, PI insurance of about $500 to $1,500/yr) dominate.
