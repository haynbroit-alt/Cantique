# Adversarial verification of Candidate B (quantum export-control and outbound-investment compliance radar) and Candidate C (quantum claims-vs-delivery intelligence for investors)

Scope and method:
- Research was done on 22 November 2026, for a founder with about zero capital, no quantum brand and no physics PhD, working mostly through AI agents.
- About 35 search and fetch calls were made. Priority went to questions 1-4.
- Every claim is dated where the source allows. "Inference" means my reasoning, not a sourced fact.
- Several facts come from search-engine summaries of pages I did not open in full. These are flagged as such and should be treated as second-hand.
- **Correction to an earlier note (04_pelles_et_pioches.md):** the $200k Amidi LLC penalty listed there under COINS/OISP concerned an embodied-AI investment, not a quantum one. To date there has been no quantum-specific OISP enforcement (see Q2).

## 1. Competitors for B: who already sells export-control, sanctions and investment-screening intelligence, and do they cover quantum?

### Takeaway
The market is crowded at both ends:
- **Free:** quantum-specific legal guides, law-firm alerts, think-tank and university guidance.
- **Paid:** outbound-investment entity data that already names quantum (Kharon, Sayari), generic restricted-party screening (Descartes, from about $3k a year), and a fast-growing set of AI export-classification tools, including free ones.

I found no paid product that is quantum-only. That looks less like an unserved gap and more like a sign that quantum alone is too small to be a standalone compliance category.

### Cited Findings

**Paid data and screening that already covers quantum outbound investment**
- **Kharon** (page originally published 16 April 2025, updated 5 November 2026) sells "OISP data" that "identifies entities worldwide operating in the technology categories covered by EO 14105", including majority-owned or controlled entities.
  - Its pre-deal screening is pitched to "identify companies that may engage in covered semiconductor, quantum, or AI activities before a transaction closes".
  - Other features: ownership-network exploration (ClearView), portfolio monitoring via data extracts (GraphCast) or an API, and research on covered sectors (CoreStream).
  - No prices and no named clients appear on the page.
  - Sources: [Kharon, OISP and COINS Act explainer](https://www.kharon.com/resources/regulatory-trends/investment-risk/outbound-investment-rule-impact-on-business); [Kharon outbound landing page](https://go.kharon.com/outboundinvestment)
- **Sayari** markets a "world model" of corporate ownership behind technology manufacturers, semiconductor firms and investment vehicles, including in China, for dual-use investment screening. Its investigation brief lists outbound investment screening among the regulations it addresses. Page dates are unclear. Sources: [Sayari, technology](https://sayari.com/enterprise/technology/); [Sayari, economic-security brief](https://sayari.com/resources/investigation-briefs/economic-security-mandate/)
- **Strider Technologies** published "Quantum Dragon" (December 2019) on China's dual-use quantum effort and sells a state-sponsored-risk data platform. No recent outbound-specific product launch was found. Sources: [Strider, Quantum Dragon](https://striderintel.com/newsroom/strider-releases-report-on-chinas-efforts-around-dual-use-quantum-technologies/); [Strider, PRC invests in quantum](https://www.striderintel.com/resources/the-prc-invests-in-quantum-technology/)

**Generic restricted-party screening (price anchor)**
- **Descartes Visual Compliance / Denied Party Screening:**
  - Descartes says pricing "starts at a few thousand dollars a year" for a basic, low-volume, single-user setup ([Descartes pricing guide](https://descartes.com/resources/blog/pricing-guide-to-denied-party-screening-software-solutions)).
  - Capterra lists $3,000 a year for single-user Basic and up to $100,000+ for enterprise. GetApp labels the $3,000 per user per year, so the unit is ambiguous. Sources: [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/266364/Descartes-Denied-Party-Screening/); [GetApp](https://www.getapp.com/all-software/a/descartes-denied-party-screening/)
- **Thomson Reuters ONESOURCE Global Trade** pushed regulatory content on the US quantum controls to customers on 10 September 2024 ([TR ONESOURCE regulatory insight](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en-us/help/onesource-global-trade/regulatory-insights/2024/september-10th/us-new-controls-quantum-computing.html)). No price was found.

**AI export-classification tools (ECCN / dual-use), most of them 2025-2026 entrants**
- Trademo, "AI Export Control & Dual-Use Classification Engine", gives recommendations with confidence scores ([Trademo](https://www.trademo.com/ai-export-control-dual-use-classification)).
- Digicust offers "possible ECCN suggestions, confidence information, and reasoning" against Commerce Control List (CCL) criteria ([Digicust](https://digicust.com/en/solutions/ai-export-control/)).
- BITE Data released an "agentic" workflow in May 2026 covering Schedule B, ITAR/USML and EAR/CCL with ECCN determination ([BITE Data](https://bitedata.io/blog/new-release-export-control-classification-compliance-workflow)).
- Exporra (AI ECCN/HS classification plus sanctions screening) said it would launch in June 2026 ([Exporra](https://www.exporra.com/)).
- ECCN.help is a free AI classifier (reportedly 5 free items per account). It is promoted by an affiliated site, so the claims are self-serving. Sources: [Medium](https://medium.com/@isurajitroy/the-best-free-eccn-classification-tool-in-2026-b0ffe914b7c8); [TariffWolf](https://tariffwolf.com/best-free-eccn-classification-tool-in-2026/)
- BIS itself tells exporters to first ask the manufacturer for the ECCN, then self-classify, or to file a free official classification request (CCATS) via SNAP-R, limited to six items per request ([BIS, Classify your item](https://bis.gov/licensing/classify-your-item)).

I read the title only.
- **Law firms with quantum industry groups** sell counsel, e.g. WSGR ([WSGR](https://www.wsgr.com/en/services/industries/quantum-computing.html)).

### Inferences
- **The candidate is boxed in on both sides:**
  - Investors get the screen they need (is this target a covered quantum entity?) from entity-data vendors (Kharon, Sayari) with ownership graphs that an AI-agent founder cannot replicate.
  - Exporters get classification help from AI classifiers (some free), BIS's free CCATS route and counsel.
  - Everyone gets plain-language briefings free from CQE, law firms and PostQuantum.
- **Sustainable room is narrow:** a cross-jurisdiction comparison of quantum control parameters, set out side by side, e.g.:
  - EU Annex I quantum entries from Delegated Regulation 2025/2003;
  - US ECCNs such as 4A906;
  - national thresholds such as Germany's (>34 qubits plus error thresholds, per the earlier note);
  - the UK, NL, ES, FR, JP and CA lists.

  None of the sources found publishes this, but it is a document, not a subscription business.
- **Generalist vendors could add a "quantum" tag cheaply.** Kharon has effectively done so for OISP.

### Gaps
- Not checked for lack of budget: Exiger, Castellum.AI, Govini, Horizon Advisory, E2open/Amber Road, SAP GTS, ECTI, Society for International Affairs, AUECO, the EU dual-use helpdesk, the UK ECJU, QED-C or GQI compliance offerings.
- No prices were found for Kharon, Sayari, Strider or ONESOURCE.
- I did not open the CQE export-controls chapter to judge its depth or update cadence.

## 2. Willingness to pay for B: evidence of pain, spending, enforcement and liability barriers

### Takeaway
The pain is real for EU and UK component exporters: lost customers, slow and unpredictable licences, high legal fees. But it is a pain about government processing times, which an information product does not fix.

On the investor side the market is tiny:
- Treasury has received only about 100 OISP notifications in total (21 months), "in the semiconductor and AI industries".
- The October 2026 proposed rule keeps quantum in the prohibited category, so there is no quantum notification workflow to sell into.
- The only penalty so far concerned AI, not quantum.

WTP evidence for quantum-specific compliance information is weak to absent.

### Cited Findings

**Investor side (US outbound rules)**
- **Volume:** Treasury's proposed rule says the program has received "approximately 100 notifications of investments in the semiconductor and AI industries" in about 21 months since 2 January 2025. The notifications came from VC firms, asset managers, investment banks and individual investors. Sources: [Federal Register / govinfo, NPRM 2026-21786, 26 Oct 2026](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-10-26/html/2026-21786.htm); [A&O Shearman](https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/treasury-proposes-significant-expansion-of-us-outbound-investment-security-program)
- **Quantum stays prohibited, not notifiable** ([NPRM text, govinfo](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-10-26/html/2026-21786.htm)). The proposed rule covers:
  - developing or producing a quantum computer or critical components, e.g. dilution refrigerators;
  - software to design, simulate, test or control a quantum computer;
  - "the development of algorithms and applications for quantum information science technologies", with an end-use limit;
  - quantum sensing and quantum networks with military, intelligence or mass-surveillance end uses.

  Comments are due 7 December 2026.
- **First OISP penalty (Amidi LLC):** $200,000, imposed July 2026 and announced 7 October 2026.
  - It was for failing to notify a roughly $92,478 investment made on 19 April 2025, through a Chinese fund subsidiary, in Shanghai Qiongche Intelligent Technology (Noematrix), an **embodied-AI** company.
  - Treasury found it through its own monitoring.
  - Sources: [MoFo, 15 Oct 2026](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/261015-treasury-announces-first-enforcement-penalty); [Paul Weiss](https://www.paulweiss.com/insights/client-memos/treasury-announces-first-ever-penalty-under-the-outbound-investment-security-program); [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/news/china/diplomacy/article/3370101/us-issues-first-outbound-investment-fine-over-chinese-robotics-ai-deal?pgtype=topic); [CLS Blue Sky / Paul Weiss, 19 Oct 2026](https://clsbluesky.law.columbia.edu/2026/10/19/paul-weiss-discusses-treasurys-first-penalty-under-outbound-investment-security-program/)
- **Reporting:** the COINS Act (enacted 18 December 2025) requires an annual Treasury report on notifications, enforcement and technology trends. The first is due by June 2027 ([CRS IF12629](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF12629); [Sidley, Jan 2026](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2026/01/the-comprehensive-outbound-investment-national-security-act-of-2025-updates)).

**Exporter side (EU and UK)**
- **QuIC paper, 15 October 2026:** QuIC (European Quantum Industry Consortium) sent *Optimising Dual-Use Controls for European Quantum Technologies* to the Commission's evaluation of Regulation 2021/821 ([PostQuantum, Oct 2026](https://postquantum.com/quantum-policy/eu-quantum-export-controls/)). As reported by PostQuantum, not the original:
  - Complaints: "long and unpredictable lead times"; member states applying rules inconsistently; "some members reported having already lost customers because of shipment delays"; others lost contracts and paid very high legal fees.
  - Customs officers bounce files back to exporters, adding weeks.
  - It contains **no** measured licensing times and no count of affected members.
  - Requests: e-licensing, published processing times, non-binding classification opinions within 30 days, EU001 extension to South Korea, a safe harbour for remote operation, and guidance on cloud access.
- **QED-C Global Quantum Supply Chain study (23 September):** 160 quantum companies in 15 countries. 90% buy from abroad and about a quarter buy from China. The analysis says the survey cannot measure the effect of export controls ([PostQuantum](https://postquantum.com/quantum-policy/quantum-supply-chain-survey/)).
  - The same search returned a 2022 QED-C/Hyperion survey of 47 mostly US firms that rated stricter export controls "very detrimental". This is second-hand from the search summary; the original was not located.
- **Small firms:** a Quantum World Congress piece describes a small hardware firm where one operations employee handles licensing alongside shipping ([QWC](https://www.quantumworldcongress.com/news/quantum-is-going-strategic-its-supply-chain-is-already-global)).
- **Licence denials (IISS analysis, reported 3 August 2026):** the UK denied a licence for Oxford Instruments equipment, and a Chinese institute reportedly obtained Bluefors dilution refrigerators from Finland before Finnish controls were fully in place ([TQI, 3 Aug 2026](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/08/03/western-quantum-export-controls-are-evolving-into-an-industrial-strategy-iiss-analysis-says/)).

**Fees and liability**
- **Classification fees:** no public fee data was found. BIS offers a free classification route, and universities say complex items "will require a skilled professional" ([BIS](https://bis.gov/licensing/classify-your-item); [UCSB classification review](https://www.research.ucsb.edu/sites/default/files/ri/UCSB-University-Export-Control/Commodity%20Classification%20Review.pdf)).
- **How vendors frame liability:** AI classification vendors frame their output as suggestions with confidence scores and reasoning, not determinations ([Digicust](https://digicust.com/en/solutions/ai-export-control/); [Trademo](https://www.trademo.com/ai-export-control-dual-use-classification)).

### Inferences
- **Investor segment (VCs and CVCs):** with quantum prohibited, the US-investor need is a binary "is the target or its family a covered Chinese (or, under the NPRM, Russian, Iranian etc.) quantum entity?".
  - US VCs almost never do such deals. The ~100 notifications in 21 months are in semis and AI, not quantum.
  - The question is answered by entity data plus counsel.
  - A quantum-specific subscription for US VCs has very few buyers and little recurring need.
  - The COINS final rule (due by 13 March 2027 per the earlier note) widens the country list. That helps the entity-data vendors more than a briefing product.
- **Exporter segment (cryogenics, lasers, control electronics):** the money is spent on lawyers for determinations and licence applications, and the main delay is government processing.
  - A founder with no compliance credential cannot sign determinations.
  - What remains is low-value monitoring, competing with free alerts.
- **Universities:** they have in-house export-control offices that already publish quantum guidance (BNL, UChicago). They are poor buyers for a startup product.
- **Liability:** an ECCN or OISP-coverage call is a legal determination.
  - The Amidi case shows penalties can exceed twice the deal value.
  - Buyers will not rely on an unknown non-lawyer brand for anything that matters. They will use it, at most, as a free pre-read.
  - Unauthorised-practice-of-law risk is manageable for "information only" products, but that framing caps the value.

### Gaps
- Not found:
  - fees for ECCN classification by consultants or law firms;
  - BIS licence processing times for quantum ECCNs;
  - any BIS enforcement case specifically about quantum items;
  - the number of quantum transactions blocked by CFIUS or EU FDI screening;
  - statements by QuIC or QED-C quantifying spending on compliance.
- UPL rules and the disclaimer language used by non-law compliance SaaS were not researched.
- Japan and China counter-measures, and the UK ECJU specifics, were not verified.

## 3. Competitors for C: who already sells independent technical and claims analysis of quantum companies?

### Takeaway
The "promised vs delivered" thesis is already being published, much of it free:
- PostQuantum.com published a full SPAC-projection vs actuals table (14 July 2026).
- Quantum Zeitgeist publishes a five-year record of the listed pure plays (September 2026, partly paid).
- Well-funded short sellers produce deep claims-checking reports (Kerrisdale, Wolfpack, Capybara, Iceberg, and in 2022 the IonQ short report).
- Institutional quantum intelligence is sold by GQI/QCR, TQI, S&P Global and Omdia.
- Sell-side coverage expanded in 2026 (Wedbush, Jefferies, Benchmark).

The core differentiator of Candidate C is therefore already a commodity.

### Cited Findings

**Short sellers (free reports, deep resources)**

  - Shares fell about 3%.

## 5. Regulatory and liability issues for C: adviser registration, financial-promotion rules, defamation

### Takeaway
In the US, an impersonal, regular, general-circulation publication is generally outside investment-adviser registration under Lowe v. SEC (1985). That protection is lost if the content becomes promotional, personalised, or tied to the publisher's own positions, and it never protects fraud or misstatements.

In the UK, the FCA's "principal purpose" test (PERG 7, article 54) governs.

The bigger practical risk is defamation and litigation from named companies:
- Quantum firms answer critics publicly (IonQ vs Wolfpack).
- At least one quantum-labelled firm has sued a short seller and Stocktwits for defamation (aggregator source).
- A tracker whose product is "company X's claim is false" invites this, and a zero-capital founder cannot fund a defence.

### Cited Findings
- **Lowe v. SEC, 472 U.S. 181 (1985):**
  - Publications that are "bona fide" and "of general and regular circulation" fall within the publisher's exclusion, so the publisher is not an "investment adviser".
  - Advice about specific securities alone does not make it personalised.
  - "Bona fide" means disinterested commentary and analysis as opposed to promotional material.
  - Sources: [Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/472/181); [govinfo US Reports](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-472/pdf/USREPORTS-472-181.pdf)
- **Limits:** a 1986 decision held that Lowe does not mean a bona fide publication "can commit fraud at will" ([SEC v. Wall Street Pub. Institute, D.D.C. 1986](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/FSupp/664/554/1504347/)).
- **Seeking Alpha (2024):** a court declined a narrow reading of the exclusion in a case involving Seeking Alpha ([Greenberg Traurig, Aug 2024](https://www.gtlaw.com/ja/insights/2024/8/no-need-for-seeking-alpha-to-seek-registration); details not verified against the opinion). Seeking Alpha's standard disclaimer says it is not a licensed securities dealer, broker or US investment adviser ([Seeking Alpha article footer](https://seekingalpha.com/article/4824751-quantum-computing-selling-more-shares-than-quantum-machines)).
- **UK, FCA PERG 7 (last updated 27 March 2026):**
  - The article 54 exclusion turns on the "principal purpose" of the publication or service.
  - A publisher can apply to the FCA for a certificate that the exclusion applies (PERG 7.6).
  - The older certificate regime ended 1 December 2001.
  - Sources: [FCA PERG 7.4](https://www.handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/PERG/7/4.html); [FCA PERG 7](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/perg7)
- **Defamation and litigation exposure:**
  - Quantum Cyber N.V. reportedly sued White Diamond Research, its analyst and Stocktwits in New York Supreme Court, seeking more than $5.69M for defamation and market manipulation. This comes from an aggregator and is unverified against the docket ([briefglance](https://briefglance.com/companies/quantum-cyber-n-v/pulses/67376); [briefglance follow-up](https://briefglance.com/companies/quantum-cyber-n-v/pulses/71317)).
  - IonQ publicly called Wolfpack's report false but no suit was found ([DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/ionq-disputes-claims-from-short-seller-that-lost-government-contracts-have-left-546m-black-hole-in-companys-revenue/)).
  - SemiAnalysis received an Arm China cease-and-desist ([Substrate](https://substratemag.com/dylanpatel/)).

### Inferences
- **Safe-ish design:**
  - Publish only sourced, dated, verbatim claims next to sourced outcomes, with no ratings or buy/sell calls.
  - Hold no positions, or disclose them strictly.
  - Use the same content for all subscribers, on a regular schedule.
  - Allow a right of reply.

  This keeps the product within the US publisher's exclusion and lowers defamation risk, but it also removes the "edge" hedge funds pay for.
- **Per-company paid dossiers to specific funds** look more like personalised research. They raise adviser and EU/UK "investment recommendation" questions. Per my background knowledge (not verified this session), EU MAR Article 20 and Delegated Regulation 2016/958 impose disclosure duties on anyone producing investment recommendations.
- **Scale of the defence problem:** short sellers manage it with lawyers and evidence files. A zero-capital founder relying on AI agents has neither, and one AI error in a published "claim vs delivery" item could be actionable.

### Gaps
- Not researched: EU MiFID II / ESMA rules on investment research by non-authorised persons; France AMF rules (including the recommandations d'investissement regime); UK financial-promotion rules (s.21 FSMA) for paid newsletters; and the details of any quantum defamation case.

## 6. AI-agent feasibility: can agents reliably maintain these trackers from the Federal Register, EUR-Lex, EDGAR and arXiv?

### Takeaway
Feasible for collection and drafting, not for unsupervised publication:
- Purpose-built legal AI tools still hallucinate on 17-33% of legal queries (Stanford, 2024-2025).
- In this session, the Federal Register website blocked an automated fetch, and the source documents are very long (a 280k-character proposed rule).
- Search-engine summaries conflated unrelated entities (the "GQI" quantum firm vs the Natixis ETF of the same ticker).

Both products need a human or expert check on every published item. That is the scarce input for this founder.

### Cited Findings
- **Stanford RegLab/HAI, "Hallucination-Free?"** (preprint 2024; Journal of Empirical Legal Studies 2025). Over 200 preregistered queries were used.

| Tool | Hallucination rate | Accurate and grounded | Incomplete answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lexis+ AI | 17% or more | 65% | 18% |
| Westlaw AI-Assisted Research | about one-third | 41% | 25% |
| Ask Practical Law AI | not given | 19% | 62% |
| General chatbots (earlier work) | 58-82% | n/a | n/a |

  LexisNexis disputed the results. Sources: [Stanford HAI](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/ai-trial-legal-models-hallucinate-1-out-6-queries); [arXiv 2405.20362](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.20362v1); [Stanford Law](https://law.stanford.edu/publications/hallucination-free/)
- **First-hand, this session:**
  - Fetching the OISP proposed rule from federalregister.gov returned a 302 redirect to `unblock.federalregister.gov`, a bot gate ([federalregister.gov document](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/26/2026-21786/outbound-investment-security-program-regulations)).
  - The govinfo.gov mirror worked, but the document was 279,635 characters, and one fetch read only the first 100,000. The burden and cost estimates were not reached ([govinfo](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-10-26/html/2026-21786.htm)).
- **Vendor framing:** AI classification vendors present outputs as "suggestions" with confidence scores, i.e. they expect human validation ([Digicust](https://digicust.com/en/solutions/ai-export-control/); [Trademo](https://www.trademo.com/ai-export-control-dual-use-classification)).

### Inferences
- **For B:**
  - Change detection (new Federal Register or EUR-Lex documents, BIS and Treasury FAQs) is a solved, agent-friendly task via official APIs and RSS. Free law-firm alerts already provide it within days.
  - The value-adding step, mapping a parameter threshold to a product, is exactly where AI error is costly and liability attaches.
- **For C:**
  - Pulling guidance from EDGAR 10-Ks and 8-Ks, and roadmap claims from press releases, is agent-friendly.
  - Judging whether an arXiv result "delivers" a qubit or fidelity promise is not. There are no standard metrics: physical vs logical qubits, two-qubit fidelity at what depth, algorithmic qubits. A wrong call is both a credibility and a defamation risk.

### Gaps
- No measured accuracy data for agents parsing EUR-Lex or arXiv quantum papers.
- Federal Register and EDGAR API rate limits were not checked in this session.

## 7. Verdict: false-good-idea risks, time to first revenue, price points, defensibility

### Takeaway
- **Candidate B should be eliminated as a standalone business.** At most it survives as a free lead magnet, or as a one-off paid document inside the grant/tender radar.
- **Candidate C should be eliminated as an investor-research business** (hedge funds, family offices, sell-side). It survives only as a low-price media or audience product built on a public, time-stamped "claims ledger", with a modest ceiling and real litigation risk.
- Neither beats the #1 radar on the founder's constraints.

### Cited Findings
- The key facts behind these verdicts:
  - about 100 OISP notifications in 21 months, none described as quantum, with quantum kept prohibited ([govinfo NPRM](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-10-26/html/2026-21786.htm));
  - the free Quantum Law Navigator ([TQI](https://thequantuminsider.com/2025/11/04/quantum-law-report-maps-legal-minefield-for-americas-quantum-tech-frontier/));
  - Kharon's quantum-aware OISP screening ([Kharon](https://www.kharon.com/resources/regulatory-trends/investment-risk/outbound-investment-rule-impact-on-business));
  - QuIC's pain centred on government lead times ([PostQuantum](https://postquantum.com/quantum-policy/eu-quantum-export-controls/));
  - the free PostQuantum projection-vs-actuals table ([PostQuantum](https://postquantum.com/quantum-commercialization/quantum-spac-wave/));
  - short-seller coverage ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/02/04/ionq-wolfpack-research-short-seller-says-quantum-computing-company-misled-investors-about-cancelled-government-backdoor-earmarks/); [Kerrisdale](https://www.kerrisdalecap.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/IonQ-%E2%80%93-Kerrisdale.pdf));
  - the structure of SemiAnalysis's revenue ([Substrate](https://substratemag.com/dylanpatel/));
  - QTUM's AUM decline ([Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/QTUM/); [Walnut](https://walnutinvest.com/etf/qtum)).

### Inferences

**Candidate B: why it is a false good idea**
1. **The buyer pool has almost no recurring need.** US investors cannot do covered quantum deals in China at all, so there is no quantum notification workflow. The ~100 notifications are in semis and AI. The decision is binary and served by entity-data vendors plus counsel.
2. **Exporters' pain is licensing lead times and legal fees.** QuIC is lobbying the Commission to fix the process. A monitoring subscription does not remove delays, and determinations require lawyers.
3. **Free supply is saturated:** CQE, law firms, Freshfields, PostQuantum, QSI, BNL, UChicago and TR ONESOURCE content.
4. **The AI classification niche is crowded and partly free** (Trademo, Digicust, BITE Data, ECCN.help, Exporra), and the generic tools can add quantum ECCNs cheaply.
5. **Liability:** a non-lawyer, no-brand founder cannot sell reliance, and penalties can be large (Amidi: 2x the deal value).

- **Time to first revenue:**
  - About 1-3 months for a one-off paid "EU/US/national quantum control-parameter cross-walk" guide (€100-500) or a webinar timed to the COINS comment deadline (7 December 2026) or the final rule (by March 2027).
  - Recurring revenue looks unlikely.
- **Plausible prices:** none supported by evidence. The anchors are Descartes at about $3k a year for generic screening and TQI datasets at $3-6k one-time.
- **Defensibility:** low. The content is public, incumbents can add a quantum tag, and the brand needs credentials.
- **Salvage:**
  - Fold a quantum-controls tracker into the grant/tender radar as free content for the same audience (quantum SMEs).
  - Or sell a white-label cross-walk to a cluster or industry association (QuIC, QED-C, national clusters) or to a law firm that signs the legal layer.

**Candidate C: why it is a false good idea**
1. **The headline insight is already public and free:** about 12 cents of revenue per projected dollar (PostQuantum, July 2026); Quantum Zeitgeist's five-year record; repeated short-seller deep dives.
2. **Hedge funds pay for proprietary edge** (expert calls at about $1,000+ per hour, alternative data, investigators). An AI-agent synthesis of public filings by a non-physicist is not edge.
3. **The technical "delivery" judgement needs physics expertise the founder lacks.** Errors are both a credibility and a defamation risk, and quantum companies push back publicly.
4. **The audience is pro-cyclical and shrinking from its 2025-2026 peak** (QTUM about $6B down to $5.1B), and retail tends to pay little.
5. **SemiAnalysis is a survivorship-biased comparable:**
   - a far larger underlying market;
   - a founder with an existing brand;
   - proprietary supply-chain methods;
   - under 5% of revenue from the newsletter.

- **Time to first revenue:** about 1-3 months for first Substack payers ($8-20 a month). Meaningful revenue (>$5k a month) likely takes 12-24 months of audience building, with no assurance.
- **Per-company dossiers:** $200-1,000 to journalists or corporate-strategy teams are plausible but unverified.
- **Defensibility:** moderate only if a structured, time-stamped claims ledger is maintained for years (it compounds and becomes citable), backed by a neutrality brand (no positions, right of reply). TQI, GQI or PostQuantum could copy it quickly.
- **Salvage:** run the claims ledger as a free public dataset and newsletter that builds audience and credibility for the founder's main business, rather than as the business itself.

**Ranking:** both B and C are dominated by the #1 grant/tender radar. If one must be kept as a secondary line, C (as a cheap media asset) is slightly more viable than B. B's buyers either have no recurring need (investors) or need lawyers and faster governments (exporters).

### Gaps
- No customer interviews or pre-sales were possible. Willingness to pay at the suggested price points is unvalidated.
- The verdict would change for B if:
  - Treasury's first COINS annual report (due June 2027) showed material quantum notification or enforcement volume; or
  - an EU or UK outbound-investment regime covering quantum were adopted with notification duties for EU funds (not researched here).
- The verdict would change for C if a quantum newsletter with verifiable paid scale (thousands of payers) were found.
