# Quantum standards, neutral data/benchmark layers, communities and leading indicators (state as of 22 Nov 2026) + historical precedents of small "neutral layer" actors

Scope note: this file covers standards, benchmarks, data/registries, community and timing signals. Hardware/software stack and the Q-Day / crypto-asset transition are covered by other researchers. All dates are those of the source; "as of 2026-11-22" means queried live by this researcher on that day. Forecasts for 2031-2041 are labelled as such.

Method note on live data pulled 2026-11-22 (primary, reproducible):
- GitHub stars via shields.io JSON endpoint (`https://img.shields.io/github/stars/<owner>/<repo>.json`); PyPI downloads via `https://pypistats.org/api/packages/<pkg>/recent` ("last_month"; includes CI/mirror traffic, so an upper bound on human use); Quantum Computing Stack Exchange via `https://api.stackexchange.com/2.3/questions?site=quantumcomputing&fromdate=…&todate=…&filter=total` and `/info`; arXiv via `https://export.arxiv.org/api/query?search_query=cat:quant-ph+AND+submittedDate:[…]` (cat: includes cross-lists; API counts are approximate).

## 1. Historical precedents: small actors who became indispensable neutral layers

### Takeaway
The neutral info/coordination layers that endured were started by 1-3 people with almost no capital, took roughly 3-7 years to become indispensable, and captured value mostly by being acquired by (or pivoting toward) a transaction business, not by selling data. Many were later captured by an interested party (Binance–CoinMarketCap, Bullish–CoinDesk, Meta–Papers with Code, Redgate–DB-Engines), which shows both the exit path and the "neutrality decay" risk. Survivorship bias is strong: this list only contains winners.

### Cited Findings
**CoinMarketCap (crypto price/rank aggregator)**
- Founded around May 2013 by a single (pseudonymous) person, Brandon Chez; started as a simple ranking of crypto market caps — [CoinMarketCap Academy](https://coinmarketcap.com/academy/article/the-untold-story-how-coinmarketcap-became-home-of-crypto); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coinmarketcap)
- Run as a side project for its first ~3 years; leadership says the first sign it "could be revenue-positive" came in 2015 when a site offered to buy all ad spots — [Coinspeaker interview with CMC leadership](https://www.coinspeaker.com/exclusive-coinmarketcap-binance); [ProductMint](https://productmint.com/coinmarketcap-business-model-how-does-coinmarketcap-make-money/)
- Today's revenue mix: advertising (main), API subscriptions, job listings, referral fees — [Dealroom](https://app.dealroom.co/companies/coinmarketcap); [ProductMint](https://productmint.com/coinmarketcap-business-model-how-does-coinmarketcap-make-money/)
- Acquired by Binance, confirmed 2 Apr 2020; price undisclosed; The Block reported "could be worth as much as $400M", another source "closer to $300M", paid mainly in equity and BNB; Chez stepped down as CEO; critics questioned independence — [The Block, deal report](https://www.theblock.co/post/60371/binance-is-set-to-acquire-coinmarketcap-the-deal-could-be-worth-as-much-as-400-million); [The Block, confirmation](https://www.theblock.co/post/60595/binance-confirms-acquiring-coinmarketcap); [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/24037/binance-confirms-major-acquistion-of-coinmarketcap)
- Binance CEO's stated rationale: "They have the users" (traffic was the asset) — [Yahoo/CoinDesk](https://sg.news.yahoo.com/binance-coinmarketcap-acquisition-bet-crypto-130003977.html)

**CoinDesk (crypto news)**
- DCG bought CoinDesk for ~$500,000 in 2016; Bullish bought it (deal completed Nov 2023, all-cash) for a reported ~$70-80M (Fortune), far below the up-to-$300M DCG had hoped for — [Fortune, Feb 2024](https://fortune.com/crypto/2024/02/13/coindesk-bullish-ftx-sale-digital-currency-group-restructuring/); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/bullish-paid-close-75-million-170506197.html); [Blockworks](https://blockworks.co/news/bullish-acquires-coindesk-dcg)

**Stack Overflow**
- Public Q&A since 2008 (Spolsky, Atwood); acquired by Prosus for $1.8B, announced 2 June 2021 — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/06/02/stack-overflow-acquired-by-prosus-for-a-reported-1-8-billion); [Stack Overflow blog](https://stackoverflow.blog/2021/06/02/prosus-acquires-stack-overflow/)

**TOP500 (ranking precedent)**
- Started at Univ. of Mannheim in early 1993 by Hans Meuer and Erich Strohmaier, with Jack Dongarra's LINPACK; published twice a year since June 1993 based only on site and vendor submissions; tied to Meuer's ISC conference; editors today Dongarra, Strohmaier, Horst Simon — [TOP500 Introduction](https://top500.org/project/introduction/); [LBL profile](https://cs.lbl.gov/news-media/news/2012/erich-strohmaier-and-the-top500-a-list-that-s-taken-on-a-life-of-its-own)

**DB-Engines (popularity ranking precedent)**
- Launched 2012 by Solid IT (Paul Andlinger, Vienna) as an internal consultancy tool that became a standalone business; assets bought by Redgate Software in June 2024 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DB-Engines_ranking); [HostingAdvice interview](https://www.hostingadvice.com/blog/the-db-engines-knowledge-base/)
- Methodology uses only public signals (web mentions, Google Trends, Stack Overflow/DBA SE activity, job ads) and explicitly does not measure installations — [DB-Engines ranking definition](https://db-engines.com/en/ranking_definition)

**DefiLlama (open DeFi data, 2020s)**
- Free, open-source per-protocol "adapters" on GitHub; paid pro tier (~$300/month API); a token plan was scrapped amid internal disputes because the project wasn't generating revenue; Oct 2025 public accusation that Blockworks resold DefiLlama's free data — [DL News](https://www.dlnews.com/articles/defi/an-interview-with-0xngmi-on-new-defillama-pro-dashboard/); [Blockworks](https://blockworks.co/news/defi-llama-quells-internal-squabbles-scraps-token-launch-plans); [defillama.com/llms.txt](https://defillama.com/llms.txt)

### Inferences
- Pattern: (1) appear when the underlying asset is tiny and ignored; (2) one person or a tiny team; (3) become the default reference in the first hype cycle (CMC ~2013→2017; HF hub ~2018→2021); (4) value is realized via traffic → advertising/API, then acquisition by a transaction-layer player. Pure data sales rarely produce large standalone outcomes (Etherscan's decade-long, bootstrapped, ~$M-scale revenue; DefiLlama's revenue struggle).
- Time-to-indispensable correlates with the speculative cycle, not with technology maturity: CMC and Blockchain.info mattered because price/attention exploded. Quantum has no retail-price cycle, so the analogous demand driver must be procurement/enterprise decisions and government programs (DARPA QBI), which are slower and more trust-sensitive.
- Neutrality is the moat but also the first thing sold: CMC (Binance), CoinDesk (DCG then Bullish), Papers with Code (Meta, then shut). A quantum neutral layer would likely be bought by a vendor, cloud marketplace or analyst firm — eroding neutrality and opening room for a successor.
- Papers with Code's 2025 closure shows a big actor can absorb then abandon a neutral layer, leaving a vacuum (lost leaderboards) — an opening for a capital-light successor in an adjacent field.
- Survivorship bias: dozens of explorers, price trackers and leaderboards from the same eras died or stayed marginal (not quantified here); CoinDesk's ~$500K 2016 price shows even "category winners" can be worth little for years.

- No quantified data on failed comparable layers (survivorship-bias denominator).

## 2. Quantum standards forming now: who drives them, openness, and whether participation can create a durable position

### Takeaway
Formal quantum standardization is early (mostly terminology, metrics, energy, QKD security), led by national bodies, big vendors and research labs; very few deliverables exist yet. De facto interfaces (OpenQASM 3, QIR, CUDA-Q/NVQLink, Stim) matter more today than formal standards, and none of them appears to have an independent conformance-testing service — that is the most concrete small-actor opening, but it requires real technical depth.

### Cited Findings
**ISO/IEC JTC 3 (Quantum technologies)**
- Secretariat BSI (UK); chair Dr Haeseong Lee (to end-2029); ad hoc groups on terminology/metrics, sensors, secure communication, computing & simulation, QRNG, enabling technologies (another version of the page lists WG 9-16 incl. supply chain and benchmarking — structure in flux) — [ISO JTC 3 committee page](https://www.iso.org/committee/10138914.html) (page returned 403 on direct fetch; content from search snapshot)
- New subcommittee JSC 3A "Quantum computing" with AFNOR (France) as secretariat, covering hardware and software for universal QC, simulators, annealers and hybrids — [ISO JTC 3](https://www.iso.org/committee/10138914.html)
- Work items approved 2026-07-08: IEC/ISO AWI 63655 "Terminology and quantities – General vocabulary"; IEC/ISO AWI TR 63742 on randomness quality/testing of quantum entropy sources — [ISO 94926](https://www.iso.org/standard/94926.html); [ISO 94930](https://www.iso.org/standard/94930.html)
- Original IEC proposal form for the JTC — [ANSI-hosted IEC proposal](https://share.ansi.org/Shared%20Documents/News%20and%20Publications/Links%20Within%20Stories/IEC%20Proposal%20Form%20-%20JTC%20on%20Quantum%20Technologies.pdf)

**IEEE**
- P7131 "Standard for Quantum Computing Performance Metrics & Performance Benchmarking": active PAR, no approved standard yet; WG chair Erik DeBenedictis; early meetings (2022) built on the QED-C benchmark suite — [IEEE SA P7131](https://standards.ieee.org/ieee/7131/10681); [P7131 WG](https://sagroups.ieee.org/7131)
- P3329 "Quantum Computing Energy Efficiency": PAR approved 15 Feb 2023; WG = Quantum Energy Initiative (Alexia Auffèves / Marco Fellous-Asiani, French-rooted); April 2026 decided not to standardize low-level (logical-gate) energy definitions in v1; debates on whether to count fabrication energy and calibration — [IEEE SA P3329](https://standards.ieee.org/ieee/3329/11162); [QEI WG](https://sagroups.ieee.org/qei); [April 2026 agenda](https://sagroups.ieee.org/qei/wp-content/uploads/sites/698/2026/04/IEEE_P3329_QEI_WG_DraftAgenda_April_2026.pdf)

**CEN-CENELEC JTC 22 (Europe)**
- Grew out of the 2020 Focus Group on Quantum Technologies (under DIN); roadmap published March 2023; JTC 22 kicked off 9 March (2023) in Berlin; plenary + 4 WGs; working documents restricted to members; July 2025: updated roadmap edition "will be released shortly" — [CEN-CENELEC quantum page](https://cencenelec.eu/areas-of-work/cen-cenelec-topics/quantum-technologies); [CEN-CENELEC 2023 news](https://www.cencenelec.eu/news-events/news/2023/brief-news/2023-03-22-standardization-for-quantum-technologies/); [CEN-CENELEC July 2025](https://www.cencenelec.eu/news-events/news/2025/brief-news/2025-07-09-cen-and-cenelec-welcome-the-european-quantum-strategy/); [DIN roadmap](https://www.din.de/resource/blob/907602/3e9dba491c9c871e708ece77ef156dc3/normungsroadmap-quantentechnologie-data.pdf)

**ETSI (QKD)**
- ETSI GS QKD 016 (2023): Common Criteria Protection Profile for prepare-and-measure QKD module pairs; certified by German BSI as BSI-CC-PP-0120-2024 (evaluated by SGS Graz), first PP under CC:2022; driven notably by Deutsche Telekom — [Telekom](https://www.telekom.com/en/newsroom/latest-updates/news/2024/2/with-patience-to-the-industry-standard); [TelecomTV](https://www.telecomtv.com/content/security/etsi-releases-world-first-protection-profile-for-quantum-key-distribution-47346/)

**IETF/IRTF QIRG (quantum internet)**
- Only two published RFCs, both informational: RFC 9340 (Mar 2023, architectural principles) and RFC 9583 (Jun 2024, application scenarios); at IETF 126 (Vienna, 21 Jul 2026): 1 RG-adopted draft (multiplane architecture) + 6 individual drafts; new individual drafts Oct-Nov 2026 (quantum-native tenets; Van Meter's Quantum Network Architecture -02) — [QIRG datatracker](https://datatracker.ietf.org/rg/qirg); [IETF 126 chair slides](https://datatracker.ietf.org/meeting/126/materials/slides-126-qirg-chair-slides-00); [RFC 9340](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/rfc9340/); [draft-van-meter-02](https://www.ietf.org/archive/id/draft-van-meter-qirg-quantum-network-architecture-02.html)

**De facto interfaces**
- QIR Alliance: launched 30 Nov 2021 under the Linux Foundation's Joint Development Foundation; founding members Honeywell (Quantinuum), Microsoft, ORNL, Quantum Circuits Inc., Rigetti — [Linux Foundation PR](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/press/press-release/new-quantum-intermediate-representation-alliance-serves-as-common-interface-for-quantum-computing-development). qir-spec repo: 107 GitHub stars (2026-11-22, shields.io).
- OpenQASM 3: governed by a Technical Steering Committee with authority over all decisions; hosted outside the LF (Qiskit/openqasm GitHub org) — [OpenQASM governance](https://github.com/openqasm/openqasm/blob/main/governance.md); [Qiskit blog on TSC](https://medium.com/qiskit/introducing-a-technical-steering-committee-for-openqasm3-f9db808108e1). Repo 1.5k stars; `openqasm3` Python package 689,365 PyPI downloads in the last month (2026-11-22, pypistats; mostly as a transitive dependency); 17.7% of 2026 Unitary survey respondents actively use OpenQASM — [QCR on 2026 survey](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/unitary-foundation-releases-2026-quantum-open-source-survey-covering-sdk-adoption-hybrid-hpc-workflows-and-ai-tooling/amp/)
- NVIDIA NVQLink: introduced Oct 2025 "for 17 quantum builders and nine scientific labs"; adopted Nov 2025 by supercomputing centres (Brookhaven, Fermilab, Berkeley Lab, Los Alamos; secondary list adds RIKEN, AIST G-QuAT, KISTI, NQCH, Pawsey, CINECA, GENCI, KAUST); Quantinuum Helios to integrate via NVQLink + CUDA-Q for QEC; `cudaq-realtime` library after March 2026; hosts validated with a latency benchmark — [NVIDIA PR Oct 2025](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-nvqlink-quantum-gpu-computing); [NVIDIA PR Nov 2025](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/scientific-supercomputing-centers-nvqlink-grace-blackwell-quantum-processors); [NVIDIA EU page](https://www.nvidia.com/en-eu/data-center/dgx-quantum/); [Converge Digest](https://convergedigest.com/nvidia-nvqlink-sees-adoption-by-national-labs/) (400 Gbps / <4 µs figures unverified on NVIDIA pages)
- QEC tooling: in 2026 survey, of QEC tool users Stim 29.8%, CUDA-Q QEC 20.8%, PyMatching 15.8%, Riverlane Deltakit 13.5% — [QCR on 2026 survey](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/unitary-foundation-releases-2026-quantum-open-source-survey-covering-sdk-adoption-hybrid-hpc-workflows-and-ai-tooling/amp/). Stim: 852 stars but 583,635 PyPI downloads last month (2026-11-22) — i.e., a de facto QEC circuit/detector-error-model format embedded in pipelines.

### Inferences
- Formal bodies (ISO/IEC, CEN-CENELEC) are accessible only through national mirror committees (e.g., AFNOR in France, which also holds the JSC 3A secretariat) and restrict documents to members; a small actor can join as a national expert, but this yields influence and credibility, not a monetizable position by itself.
- IETF/IRTF (open, free participation by individuals — general knowledge, not re-verified here) and IEEE individual-method WGs are the most open venues; quantum-internet drafts are still research-grade (2 informational RFCs in 3+ years), i.e., very early.
- Durable positions historically come from reference implementations and conformance tests (e.g., the role of test suites in web/PKI standards), not from authorship. Candidate gaps found: no independent OpenQASM 3 / QIR conformance suite or certification service, no neutral NVQLink latency-validation lab, no neutral "energy-per-useful-result" reporting under P3329, no public tracker of JTC 3/JTC 22/IEEE work-item status. These are inferences from absence in searches, not proven absences.
- Vendor gravity is strong: OpenQASM is IBM-originated, QIR Microsoft-originated, NVQLink/CUDA-Q NVIDIA-owned; a conformance actor must be trusted by all sides (credentials/institutional backing likely required, cf. LNE's role in BACQ below).

### Gaps
- Full JTC 3 work programme and member-country count (ISO page 403 on fetch); cost of joining AFNOR/BSI mirror committees; IEEE SA membership/balloting costs — not verified.
- Status of IEEE P7130 (terminology), P3155, P3120, P3185 — not found in searches.
- ITU-T quantum (Y.3800 series QKD networks) and Quantum Internet Alliance prototype network status — not researched (budget).
- Whether any OpenQASM/QIR conformance test suite exists inside vendor repos — not verified.

## 3. Benchmarks, rankings and neutral data: is there a "TOP500 / CoinMarketCap of quantum"?

### Takeaway
A nonprofit "TOP500-like" layer is forming (Unitary Foundation's Metriq relaunched March 2026 with a composite Metriq Score) and a vendor-convened but open "advantage ledger" exists (Quantum Advantage Tracker, Nov 2025), plus government-grade verification (DARPA QBI) and national projects (BACQ). But adoption is tiny (metriq-gym: 33 stars, 171 PyPI downloads/month), coverage is ~11 devices, and nothing tracks prices, roadmap delivery or cost-per-result continuously. Market intelligence is already monetized by venture-backed analysts (The Quantum Insider/Resonance, QCR/GQI, MIT QIR, McKinsey).

### Cited Findings
- DARPA QBI: goal is to determine whether any approach can reach utility scale (value exceeds cost) by 2033; >20 companies evaluated since mid-2024; 11 entered Stage B in Nov 2025; on 7 Oct 2026 Atom Computing, Diraq, IBM and IonQ advanced to Stage C (joining Microsoft and PsiQuantum from US2QC); Stage C = government evaluation team testing whether utility-scale designs can be built ("from plans to proof"); Quandela joined Stage A in May 2026 — [DARPA, 7 Oct 2026](https://www.darpa.mil/news/2026/four-more-teams-enter-quantum-benchmarking-initiative-final-stage); [DARPA Stage B](https://www.darpa.mil/research/programs/quantum-benchmarking-initiative/stage-b-selection)
- Stage B participants not (yet) in Stage C: Nord Quantique, Photonic, Quantinuum, Quantum Motion, QuEra, Silicon Quantum Computing, Xanadu — [DataCenterDynamics](https://datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/darpa-announces-four-companies-advancing-to-final-stage-of-its-quantum-benchmarking-initiative)
- Metriq relaunch (10 Mar 2026): metriq-gym (runner), metriq-data (public versioned dataset), metriq-web (beta dashboards at metriq.info); 8 benchmarks "v1.0"; 11 devices from IBM, Quantinuum, IQM, Rigetti, OriginQ; composite "Metriq Score" ("meant to start conversations, not end them"); independent project of nonprofit Unitary Foundation; open RFCs and an Open Quantum Benchmark Committee; anyone can submit via pull request; no business model stated — [Unitary Foundation post](https://unitary.foundation/posts/2026_metriq_platform/); [arXiv:2603.08680](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08680) (authors incl. W. Zeng, N. Shammah, T. Lubinski)
- Original Metriq launched May 2022 — [The Quantum Insider 2022](https://thequantuminsider.com/2022/05/18/online-platform-offers-access-to-quantum-technology-benchmarks/)
- Adoption (2026-11-22, live): metriq-gym 33 GitHub stars and 171 PyPI downloads last month; Mitiq 448 stars / 4,713 downloads — shields.io, pypistats (method above)
- QED-C application-oriented benchmarks (SRI-hosted repo, 187 stars) — maintainers state they are "not intended to be viewed as performance standards" — [GitHub QED-C benchmarks](https://github.com/SRI-International/QC-App-Oriented-Benchmarks)
- BACQ (France): €4M, Sept 2023-Aug 2026; Thales (coordinator), Eviden, CEA, CNRS, Teratec, LNE; LNE guarantees impartiality as trusted third party; adopted Eviden's Q-score (Max-Cut) as first metric; families: optimization, linear systems, physics simulation — [LNE BACQ](https://www.lne.fr/en/metriqs-france/projet-bacq); [arXiv:2403.12205](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12205); [Eviden Q-score](https://eviden.com/solutions/quantum-computing/benchmarks-for-application-centric-quantum-computing)

## 5. Timelines and leading indicators of the "explosion" moment

### Takeaway
Consensus vendor targets put early fault-tolerant machines in 2029-2030 (IBM Starling 2029: 200 logical qubits/100M gates), DARPA tests utility-scale feasibility by 2033, and experts give a 28-49% chance of a cryptographically relevant QC within 10 years (51-70% within 15). 2026 already brought contested "quantum advantage" claims, 70-logical-qubit circuits and record logical error rates, so the information-layer window is open now; the decisive observable indicators are logical-qubit counts × logical error rates, QBI stage advances, verified (unrebutted) advantage entries, and price-per-logical-operation.

### Cited Findings
- IBM: Starling (Poughkeepsie) by 2029 — 100M gates on 200 logical qubits; 2026 milestone: demonstrate quantum advantage with HPC, Nighthawk up to 360 qubits — [IBM large-scale FTQC blog](https://www.ibm.com/quantum/blog/large-scale-ftqc); [IBM roadmap 2029](https://ibm.com/roadmaps/quantum/2029); [IBM roadmap 2026](https://ibm.com/roadmaps/quantum/2026)
- 30 Jul 2026: IBM + Algorithmiq claim quantum advantage; IBM + UChicago ran 70 logical qubits, 2,415 logical two-qubit ops and 468 logical T gates with logical error ~10x below physical — [IBM newsroom (Algorithmiq)](https://newsroom.ibm.com/2026-07-30-ibm-and-algorithmiq-demonstrate-quantum-advantage,-establishing-a-framework-for-trusted-quantum-computation-beyond-classical-verification); [IBM newsroom (UChicago)](https://newsroom.ibm.com/2026-07-30-ibm-and-the-university-of-chicago-demonstrate-quantum-advantage,-establishing-trusted-quantum-computation-on-logical-circuits); [Next Platform](https://www.nextplatform.com/compute/2026/07/31/ibm-three-demonstrations-prove-quantum-advantage-has-been-reached/5282091); contested by [PostQuantum](https://postquantum.com/industry-news/ibm-three-quantum-advantage-papers/)

### Inferences
- Leading-indicator dashboard (observable, publicly sourced, automatable with AI agents):
  1. Logical qubits × logical error rate × logical depth (e.g., IBM 70 LQ, Quantinuum 5e-6/round) — the single best technical indicator; a "Moore's-law chart of logical operations" (MegaQuOp → GigaQuOp) does not appear to exist as a maintained public dataset.
  2. DARPA QBI stage transitions (Stage C entries; any 2027+ "passes") — the most credible third-party verdict.
  3. Quantum Advantage Tracker: count of Active vs. Superseded candidates and survival time before classical rebuttal.
  4. Roadmap delivery ratio (milestones delivered on time / announced) per vendor.
  5. Cloud price per shot/minute and, later, per logical operation; free-tier minutes.
  6. Developer signals: PyPI downloads (qiskit, cudaq, stim), survey size, arXiv quant-ph volume, QEC paper counts.
  7. Government procurement/standards milestones (JTC 3 first IS, CEN-CENELEC roadmap v2, ETSI-certified QKD products).
- Timing vs. Bitcoin analogy: by money and institutions ($10.9B/yr, DARPA, IBM/NVIDIA), quantum in 2026 looks less like Bitcoin 2009-2012 (zero institutional attention) and more like crypto ~2014-2016 or AI ~2015-2017 (heavy institutional funding, few real users). The information layers are thus less "empty" than in 2013, but the verification layer (who is actually delivering) is still weakly owned.
- Explosion window (FORECAST, inference): first verifiable, unrebutted application advantage ~2026-2029; early FTQC 2029-2031 per vendor roadmaps; utility-scale assessment 2033 (DARPA); CRQC probability passes 50% around 2036-2041 per GRI experts. A neutral data layer started in 2026-2027 would have ~3-5 years to build history and trust before the FTQC demand spike — comparable to CMC's 2013→2017 run.

## 6. Low-cost early bets for a capital-light founder (no physics PhD, AI agents), 2031-2041 horizon

### Takeaway
The most defensible capital-light bets are verification and data layers that incumbents structurally avoid: a continuously updated, source-linked "roadmap-delivery and claims ledger," a normalized QPU price/cost-per-result index, and Papers-with-Code-style SOTA tables for quantum (logical-qubit metrics, resource estimates), exposed as an open dataset + paid API + agent-native interfaces (MCP/llms.txt). Conformance testing for OpenQASM 3/QIR is higher-value but needs technical credibility. Main failure modes: a well-funded actor gives it away (Metriq, IBM tracker, HF), vendor capture/acquisition, and the market staying too small until ~2030.

### Cited Findings (evidence of demand/analogous success, reused from sections above)

### Inferences (ranked candidate positions; all are inferences, not proven markets)
1. **Roadmap-delivery & claims ledger ("who delivered what, when, verified by whom")** — every vendor milestone (qubits, logical qubits, fidelities, dates), advantage claims and their rebuttals, QBI stages, with source links and timestamps. Unowned (none found); AI agents can parse press releases/arXiv; the value compounds with history (like CMC's price history). Monetize: free site + paid API/alerts for investors, procurement, analysts; later sponsorship. Prerequisite: strict neutrality policy, public methodology, advisory board. Fails if: TQI/GQI or Metriq add it; vendors pressure/litigate; few paying users before ~2029.
2. **QPU price & cost-per-result index** — normalize per-minute/per-shot/per-gate-shot list prices into cost-per-benchmark-circuit (could reuse Metriq benchmark definitions) and track over time; later cost-per-logical-operation. CMC analog (price as first-class data). Fails if: enterprise prices are private contracts; cloud marketplaces (Braket, qBraid, Strangeworks) publish their own comparators; volumes too small.
3. **"Papers with Code for quantum" (SOTA tables from quant-ph)** — extract metrics (logical error rates, Λ, code distance, QV, resource estimates for RSA-2048/FeMoco) from ~16-21k papers/yr with AI, human-verified; leaderboards per task. Analog: PwC (absorbed and shut in 2025). Fails if: Hugging Face or Unitary Foundation do it free; extraction errors destroy trust; needs domain reviewers (credentials partially needed).
4. **Agent-native quantum data layer** — MCP server/llms.txt/API exposing the above as verified facts for AI coding assistants. Cheap to build; aligns with the AI-first developer base. Fails if: vendors' own docs/MCP servers win; low willingness to pay.
5. **Conformance/test suites (OpenQASM 3, QIR; later NVQLink latency, P3329 energy reporting)** — reference test corpus + public compatibility matrix across compilers/backends. High durability if adopted by the TSC/alliance; requires serious compiler expertise (AI agents help but credibility gate is high). Fails if: TSC/vendors build their own; standard churn.
6. **Standards & policy tracker (JTC 3, JSC 3A, CEN-CENELEC JTC 22, IEEE P7131/P3329, ETSI QKD, IETF QIRG)** — status of each work item, plus joining national mirror committees (e.g., AFNOR, which holds the JSC 3A secretariat) to gain insider credibility. Low capital, but small niche market (consultancy-like revenue).
7. **Not recommended:** a human Q&A/community hub (QCSE questions −58% 2021→2025, −88% to 2026 YTD); a generic news site (TQI, QCR, Quantum Zeitgeist incumbents); a competing composite hardware score (Metriq's slot).
- Cross-cutting design for "being in position before the explosion": start with open data + public methodology to earn neutrality (Let's Encrypt/DefiLlama style), accumulate time-series history from day one (non-reproducible moat), make data agent-readable, keep costs near zero until demand signals fire (QBI Stage C results, unrebutted advantage, FTQC deliveries 2029-2031), and plan for the likely exit (analyst firm, cloud marketplace or vendor) while knowing an acquisition by a vendor would destroy the neutrality that made it valuable.
- Key timing risk: if FTQC slips past ~2033 (DARPA's assessment horizon) a solo founder may run out of runway; mitigate with adjacent revenue (PQC/quantum-readiness data for enterprises, covered by other researchers) or nonprofit/grant funding (Unitary Foundation microgrants, EU/France 2030 programmes — not verified here).
